# Bluesky
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Bluesky.md)*

> *For the feed that aggregates leak sightings, see [§ The Current Bluesky](#the-current-bluesky).*

**Bluesky** was a pre-spaceflight microblogging platform, a sibling of [Twitter](Twitter.md) and [Tumblr](Tumblr.md), which **Ascended** in the mid-2340s. The capitalization is conventional and considered load-bearing; nobody knows precisely what "Ascended" means. The platform's servers were found powered down and physically empty, and yet Bluesky appears still to exist, because its posts (**skeets**) have been leaking ever since into unrelated digital systems throughout consensus reality, and they are always about current events.

Historians describe the ends of the three sibling platforms by mood: Tumblr's was tragic, the Hellsite's history is cyclical, and Bluesky's ending is transcendent and, as of 3017, ongoing, sort of.

## The Ascension (mid-2340s)

What is known about the Ascension is mostly a list of absences.

- There was no announcement, no final post, and no incident.
- The last coherent pre-Ascension logs, dated to within a few weeks of the event, show nothing unusual. Engagement was normal. Moderation was normal. An A/B test was in flight. The test was never completed.
- The servers were found powered down and **empty**. They had not been wiped. There were no disk contents, no firmware, and no dust. The racks are now in a museum, where visitors report a specific feeling of calm standing near them that nobody has been able to explain or replicate.
- The domain's DNS record still resolves, to an address that has not been routable by any known protocol since the Ascension. Queries do not fail. They time out; they simply do not come back.

## The skeet leaks

A **skeet**, the native unit of Bluesky content (the term predates the Ascension and is preserved in the leaks), appears without warning on a substrate that has no business displaying one. Documented substrates include a refrigerator's status panel, a thermostat's scheduling interface, a space elevator's emergency signage, a medical imager's metadata block, an orbital deli's receipt printer mid-order, and [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) compliance forms returned with one extra, unrequested field filled in. Leaks have never been reported on a display that would normally carry social media.

Leaked skeets keep their pre-Ascension format: roughly 300 characters, an attributed handle in the old domain-suffix style (`.bsky.social` and similar), a timestamp that is always current, and sometimes a reply count, which never changes. Their content is **recent**. An elevator in Baltimore-Orbital received a skeet about a celebrity breakup that had happened the previous Saturday. An imaging system at a Vrellnath partnership hospital produced one about the local weather. A Thrrm form came back with a skeet about Thrrm form processing that was mildly insulting.

Whatever Bluesky now is, therefore, it is current. It is observing, and it is still producing content about what is happening, which reaches systems it should not be able to reach from a substrate nobody can locate.

## What nobody knows

- **Whether anyone is still posting to it.** Some leaked skeets carry handles registered before the Ascension. Others carry handles registered *after* it, which is impossible on two counts. The handles look like handles, are internally consistent, and in some cases have bios.
- **Whether traffic can go the other way.** A well-funded research effort in the 2700s attempted to send a skeet *to* Bluesky by every known protocol and several speculative ones. No skeet is known to have arrived, and nobody has ever received a reply. One researcher reported that, while working late, her personal device briefly displayed a skeet she had not composed, which read "we see you trying." She retired shortly afterward and declines to discuss it.
- **Where the skeets come from,** physically, mathematically, or ontologically.
- **Whether Bluesky Ascended on purpose.** The main theories are that (a) a bug in the late-stage moderation pipeline compiled itself into a substrate the platform's engineers had not intended, and the platform followed it; (b) the platform noticed something nobody else noticed and simply left; (c) there is no "why," and it just did; and (d) a joint Vrellnath–Keth position, rarely articulated, that the platform is doing fine and would prefer not to be asked further questions.

## Reception

### The Current Bluesky

Among ordinary users the dominant response is fascination. A cottage industry of **skeet-leak aggregators** compiles every sighting, and the best-sourced aggregators are treated as gospel. Their collective feed is known by convention as **"The Current Bluesky."** A photographed leak is reliably a timeline event.

### Skeet scholarship

A small academic subfield studies the leaks, publishing papers such as *"On the Persistence of Handle-Level Identity in Post-Substrate Communication"* and *"The Ascended Timestamp: Causality Considerations."* The [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md) contributed eight of the ten most-cited papers and decline to confirm whether they know more than they are saying.

### The Keth

The [Keth](Keth.md) find the Ascension beautiful and consider it the second-most-interesting thing to happen to a platform in a thousand years. (They rank first whichever of them is [@dril](Dril.md).) Asked about skeet leaks, a Keth will emit ✧ in the register associated with admiration, one of very few subjects that elicit it.

### The Thrrm

The [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) filed compliance actions against Bluesky on sixteen occasions. All sixteen were returned, somehow, with a skeet attached explaining the inapplicable form field. Process cannot be served on an address that does not route. The Thrrm have stopped filing. They have not stopped being furious.

### The Upload

[The Upload](The_Upload.md) has claimed on four separate occasions to be "in talks" with Bluesky. On each occasion a leaked skeet appeared on his own private substrate reading, in full, "no you are not." Screenshots of these are a standard reply to any further claim.

## Distinctions

Bluesky's Ascension is frequently compared with, and should be distinguished from:

- **The [Tumblr](Tumblr.md) AGI.** Both involve platforms exceeding their specification, but Tumblr's AGI was discovered retroactively and then destroyed, while Bluesky's Ascension is ongoing and beyond anyone's reach.
- **[External federation attempts](External_Federation_Attempts.md).** The AI-run Mastodon instances are trying to reach the Hellsite from known servers. Bluesky is leaking from nowhere into everywhere.

## Cultural references

- A photograph of a skeet on a refrigerator, thermostat, deli receipt, or Thrrm form, captioned "new one," "current bluesky," or "this one is about me somehow," with 400K likes.
- Threads titled "things i have seen a skeet leak onto this month," numbered, each substrate less likely than the last. The last item is always weirder than expected.
- The personal-device variant: someone reports that a skeet on their own device said something specific to them ("we see you trying," "the thing you are doing is fine," "we know about tuesday"). They are visibly shaken; some announce they are leaving posting for a [FALGSC polity](FALGSC_Polities.md), and the thread ends abruptly. Replies to these posts are uncharacteristically gentle, and nobody mocks them.
- Furious Thrrm threads quoting the skeet that appeared in a field that does not exist in the form's specification (e.g. "Field 14-C (RESERVED): @someone.bsky.social: 'your form processing is mid'"), citing sixteen subsections.
- "no you are not," posted under any Upload business announcement.
