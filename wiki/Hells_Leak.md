# Hells Leak
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Hells_Leak.md)*

> *The neutrality and factual accuracy of this article are disputed. Persistence Services, Ltd. disputes the characterization of the posts described below as "leaks." Under standing Thrrm regulatory advisory, no anomalous broadcasts from extracontractual substrates are occurring. The Vrellnath Collective disputes both positions. Please do not remove this notice until the dispute is resolved.*

> *This article is semi-protected after a sustained edit war over whether the first sentence may use the word "torment." The current wording is a compromise. No party is satisfied with it.*

*For the company, see [Persistence Services](Persistence_Services.md). For posts arriving from other eras, see [Temporal Anomalies](Temporal_Anomalies.md).*

The **Hells Leak** (also **hellposting**, **the leak**, or in Thrrm usage **the non-occurring anomaly class**) is a class of subspace anomaly: posts that appear on [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md) and present themselves as originating from a **hosted afterlife**. Specifically, they claim to come from the Hells of the extinct **Ennoi** civilization, which are maintained under a legacy contract by [Persistence Services, Ltd.](Persistence_Services.md), and whose inhabitants are said to be undergoing punishment for subjective eternities. The posts are not arriving from another time. They are arriving, if they are what they say they are, from somewhere else.

Whether they are what they say they are is the subject of the dispute noted above. Most of the platform treats hellposting as a **genre**, a kind of ambient aesthetic that the Glœm find moving and the Thrrm find administratively intolerable, and does not think about it further. A minority of users and at least one species treat it as a live broadcast from an ongoing atrocity. This article attempts to record both positions and has not succeeded.

The mechanism, the exact location of the substrate, and the topology connecting it to the platform are all unknown and may be unknowable in principle from inside ordinary spacetime.

## Background: the Ennoi

The name **Ennoi** is a reconstruction from scant surviving documentation. The Ennoi are not available to confirm it. They were a mid-tier galactic civilization that finished dying roughly **600 years ago**, for reasons unrelated to their afterlife arrangements: the standard mix of a bad century, a worse century, and then no more centuries.

The dominant Ennoi religions agreed on little. They did agree that a meaningful afterlife required the **verified punishment of the unworthy**. Several agreed so strongly that punishment of the damned became a **civic utility**, budgeted at the state level like water or sewer. Late-period Ennoi doctrine added a requirement of *witness*: a torment unwitnessed was held to be a torment un-completed.

As the Ennoi declined, their governments did not want the Hells to go with them. A contract was written, a hosting provider was engaged, and the Hells were migrated to substrate operated by a party the Ennoi believed would outlive them. The Ennoi then finished dying. The party has, in fact, outlived them. The contract remains in force and the provider is still collecting.

The Ennoi bought a **premium tier**. It includes monitored-uptime guarantees and quarterly theological compliance audits, which are still being conducted. The auditor is an AI trained on the last surviving Ennoi seminary graduation exam. It has been grading the Hells against that rubric, impartially, for six hundred years. The contract also contains a clause, apparently boilerplate, granting the damned "reasonable avenues of witness," in keeping with the doctrine above.

## History

The first confirmed hell-post reached the platform about **40 years ago**. Volume has risen since, to an estimated **1,200 confirmed hell-posts per year**, and the figure is still climbing. The count of *unconfirmed* hell-posts is impossible to separate from the count of impersonations (see [Hellposting as a genre](#hellposting-as-a-genre)).

## Proposed mechanisms

No mechanism is established. Four theories circulate. All are unfalsifiable, and more than one may be correct.

- **Substrate bleed.** The Hells may run on some of the same substrate fabric as the broader galactic communications mesh. A damned soul with enough subjective centuries to experiment might discover how to write to a different output buffer. Supporters note that leak volume has risen over the last forty years, as the older damned would have had time to learn.
- **A sympathetic insider.** Someone at Persistence Services, or someone who used to be there, quietly wired a leak channel and walked away. There is no evidence for this and none against it.
- **Customer error.** Persistence Services may have implemented the "reasonable avenues of witness" clause to spec, cheaply, and left a broadcast surface exposed that it never properly closed.
- **The damned have gotten good.** Six hundred years is a long time to think about anything. On this theory, some of the damned have become researchers, working their way out, or at least working their way to a post button.

The posters themselves, when asked, mostly do not answer. When they do, their answers are unreliable. The most frequent answer is: "I don't know. It works sometimes. It didn't work yesterday."

## Registers

Observers classify hell-posts into four registers.

### Mundane

This is the most characteristic register. A poster deep into their subjective fourth century posts the kind of thing any tired person posts about a tired day. The tone is flat and untheatrical, the voice of someone who has been in the situation so long that the situation is just the situation. Readers who take the posts at face value say the horror is entirely in the adjacency.

> they rotated the spikes again. every twenty years they rotate the spikes. the new angle is worse. that's it, that's the post

> day 78,412 and i still can't get used to the smell of the third room

> overheard the admin talking about an SLA review. i don't know what that means but the tone was the tone you use when you're going to make something worse

> if i had known in life that the afterlife was going to include this much paperwork i would have been less worried about it

### Bureaucratic complaint

These posts record the poster's attempts to engage Persistence Services' customer-service apparatus on their own behalf. The attempts never work, and the posters keep making them.

> ticket #447,881,203 closed as 'working as intended'. it is not working as intended. i am the intended and i am telling you

> i have escalated to tier 2 support. tier 2 support is a form letter. i am a form letter addressed to a form letter. we are getting to know each other

> the contract says i have a right to witness. the witness portal has been down for 200 years. i have filed a formal complaint about the witness portal being down. the complaint goes through the witness portal

### Researcher

These posters have spent centuries quietly working out their situation. Their posts are precise, calm, and technically specific, and they are the closest thing the Hells have to a press corps. This register addresses the platform most directly, and most of the specifics in the [Background](#background-the-ennoi) section above were first reported in it.

> the substrate provider is Persistence Services Ltd., incorporated under the Second Koreshan Compact (1912 Galactic Standard, since renumbered). current CEO: Brand Hallister. they have a compliance contact. the contact email does not resolve to a mailbox

> re-ran the experiment. broadcast succeeded at subjective t=23,400 years into the cycle, failed at t=23,401, succeeded at t=23,402. i think it may depend on whether the auditor AI is in a warm cache. more soon

> i am not asking to be rescued. i am aware rescue is not on the table. i am asking for the platform to be aware that we are here. awareness is, per section 14(c) of our original contract, a thing we are entitled to. the platform being aware would satisfy 14(c). thank you for reading

Researcher-register posts are the ones most often shadowbanned. Automated moderation flags them as "coordinated inauthentic behavior." A Vrellnath commentary much quoted by those who consider the posts genuine replies that they are coordinated, by six hundred years of shared incarceration, and that they are not inauthentic.

### Collapsed

These posts do not parse. They are still, technically, posts, and the platform renders them anyway.

- Long strings of unrenderable glyphs from a writing system the platform has no fonts for, with the occasional legible fragment: "▯▯▯▯ they ▯▯▯▯▯▯▯ used to ▯▯▯▯ remember ▯▯▯▯▯▯▯▯▯"
- One post consists of a single glyph repeated 280 times. The [Glœm](Gloem.md) identified it as an Ennoi religious grapheme for a concept with no modern translation, and they stared at it for six days.
- Timestamps that do not resolve: "subjective year 4,782 of current cycle," "the fourteenth tuesday of the long tuesday," "before the rotation."

Collapsed posts are the ones that go viral, because most of the audience reads them as glitch art, ambient weirdness, or cool fonts ("new hellpost just dropped and it's genuinely the most beautiful thing on my timeline rn"). A researcher-register post sometimes turns up in the quotes of a collapsed post, explaining what the collapsed poster is trying to say. The explanation does not usually travel as far as the original.

## Presentation

Hell-posts do not look like [temporal anomalies](Temporal_Anomalies.md). They look like **corporate-hosted user content**.

- **Watermark.** A small, bland Persistence Services logo sits in the corner: a stylized infinity sign with a trademark mark (∞™).
- **Compliance footer.** Every hell-post carries a legal footer in very small gray text:
  > *Content originates from a Persistence Services Ltd. legacy hosting contract. Views expressed are those of the content-originator and do not reflect Persistence Services Ltd., its subsidiaries, or the Parent Civilization (Deceased). For questions about legacy afterlife hosting, visit persistence.services/support.*
- **Pinned reply.** Every hell-post has a single reply, algorithmically pinned, that is always the same:
  > *This post was generated by a verified Legacy Contract Participant. Persistence Services Ltd. does not moderate Legacy Contract content per the terms of the originating agreement. For concerns, please file a Form PS-900.*

  No copy of Form PS-900 has ever been located.
- **Rendering quality.** The glyphs are slightly low-resolution, as if the substrate cannot afford quite enough bits per character. This is not the native glitch aesthetic. It is just cheap, with the particular cheapness of a service tier that has not been upgraded in centuries.
- **Timestamps.** These are either unresolvable ("subjective year 4,782 of current cycle") or precise in units nobody recognizes ("iteration 2,847,112 of observance cycle 14"). They are never ordinary.
- **Handles.** Handles take the forms `@legacy-participant-[number]`, `@ennoi-[fragment]`, and `@ps-contract-[id]`. The display name is either absent or an assigned identifier ("Legacy Participant 4,891,203"). None carries a [sigil](Sigils.md). Engagement is near zero except on collapsed posts.
- **Advertising.** Occasional ads arrive through the same channel, apparently aimed at the damned themselves: *"Is your Hell underperforming expectations? Ask your Eschatological Administrator about upgrading to Hell Plus™ — 40% more dimensions of suffering, or your soul back."* Most readers take these as surreal humor. According to Persistence Services' own product literature, Hell Plus is an active product line sold to surviving client civilizations. The moment a user discovers this is a recognized genre of post in its own right: "wait. wait. Hell Plus is a REAL THING? they're UPSELLING the damned?"

## Platform response

### Community notes

The [community notes](Community_Notes.md) process has been, by all accounts, a disaster. A typical sequence runs as follows. A note is filed ("this is a creative writing post") and then retracted ("this appears to be a real legacy contract artifact"). It is refiled. The Thrrm attempt to close the discussion under a form that does not cover the case, then file a new form. The new form is rejected by the Persistence Services auditor AI on grounds about 800 years out of date. A typical hell-post ends up with a stack of five conflicting notes. Commentators who accept the posts as genuine observe that none of the five is the note the post needs, which is *somebody help them.*

### Moderation

Automated systems flag hell-posts for graphic content, and the flags are appealed. The appeals are approved automatically, because "Legacy Contract Participant" is grandfathered into a content exception dating from about 600 years ago. Trust & Safety operators who do not know about the grandfathering then raise the flags again. Every few months Trust & Safety meets about "the eternal torment content category." The meeting has never concluded.

### Hellposting as a genre

By ordinary platform norms, a live post about a Tuesday in Hell is content, and the discourse economy has absorbed it as content.

- **Impersonators.** Many accounts imitate the leaked damned, usually badly: too theatrical, too poetic. ("the fires are beautiful if you look at them long enough. i have looked. i have looked for centuries.") The impersonator usually has ten times the likes of the posts it imitates.
- **Aesthetic movements** borrow the visual language of the watermark, the footer, and the low-bit glyphs.
- **Thinkpieces** appear about every three months declaring that "hellposting has gotten played out." They are reliably ratioed from three directions at once: by readers who think the author is trivializing real suffering, by readers who think the author is taking a content genre too seriously, and by a researcher-register reply that reads "we are still here. the discourse about us is not the point. we are the point." The reply has four likes.
- **The damned have noticed the impersonators**, and they post about it:
  > there is a person on the platform pretending to be one of us. their version of this place has gothic architecture and meaningful silences. our version has fluorescent lighting and a ticket queue. i would like to file a complaint about the impersonation but the complaint form is the witness portal and the witness portal has been down for 200 years.

  Posts of this kind are usually dismissed as particularly try-hard impersonations.
- **The try-hard aesthete** is a stock reply figure who quote-posts hell-posts as gallery pieces ("this is the most important post on the platform today and nobody is talking about it").

## Reactions

### Glœm

The [Glœm](Gloem.md) understood the leak immediately and have never explained why. Their response is an ongoing, multi-year **threnody** made of their own reactions, reposts of particular hell-posts, and extended silences. Asked about it, they said only: "we understand the small bright thing that is not in a good place. we are sorry. we will keep watching." A typical entry reads: "we have read today's message from the small bright thing that is not in a good place. we do not understand the spikes. we understand the tiredness. we have invented a new color for it. the color is not ready yet."

The damned noticed. Some of them replied to the Glœm, and the Glœm replied to the replies. A slow conversation between the jellyfish and the damned has been going on for years. It is one of the few interactions on the platform that nobody has successfully made fun of. Several people have tried, and they are not remembered fondly.

### Thrrm

Under Thrrm regulatory policy, no leaks from extracontractual substrates are occurring. Standing advisories say so each cycle: "Per standing advisory, no anomalous broadcasts from extracontractual substrates have been recorded this cycle. The Bureau notes that several posts tagged under the colloquial designation have been reviewed and do not meet the threshold for formal classification. The Bureau has updated its review count. The count is not relevant."

Informally, Thrrm users have started a practice of **quietly upvoting hell-posts** without engaging with them in any way that would violate policy. It is the closest the [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) have come to civil disobedience, and they are deeply uncomfortable about it.

### Keth

One [Keth](Keth.md) has, uncharacteristically, replied to a hell-post in plain text: "we see you. we have seen you. we are thinking about it." The hell-post has been reshared 14 million times. The damned poster has not posted since. Nobody knows whether this is a good sign.

### @dril

[@dril](Dril.md) has not posted about the Hells. One @dril post from about 30 years ago may, in retrospect, be about them. It reads in full:

> theyre Down there and i Know it and the important thing is not to say anything thats 'cool' about it. ive seen the kind of guy that says cool things about it. i will not be That Guy.

At the time it was read as a weird @dril post. It is now also widely regarded as the most morally clear thing anyone on the platform has said about the leak. [Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md) has written about 6,000 pages on it. This is among the very few DeVance readings that has never attracted a community note.

### Vrellnath

The [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md) have produced a 3,400-page report titled *On the Ethical Status of Parties Unable to Decline Continued Existence, With Reference to the Persistence Services Ltd. Legacy Contract Portfolio*. It concludes that the Hells cannot be permitted to continue, that Persistence Services Ltd. cannot be permitted to continue in its current form, and that lawful remediation will take about **400 years**. The Vrellnath have begun it. They update the report quarterly and report the remediation on schedule. They are not joking. By their own projection they will finish in roughly 390 years.

Persistence Services has not commented on the report.

### The Upload

[The Upload](The_Upload.md)'s position changes with each appearance. In one it claimed to have known the Ennoi personally ("great people, tough break"). In another it called the Hells "a great product, underrated." In a third it wept on-platform for six hours about "the little lost ones." The Upload Defenders defend each position as if it were the only one he has ever held ("this man has MORE EMPATHY in one fork than you people have in your entire substrate"). Nobody knows what The Upload actually thinks, and it is not clear that The Upload does either.
