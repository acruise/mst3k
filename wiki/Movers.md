# Movers
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Movers.md)*

*For the platform-native entities often discussed alongside the Movers, see [Drifters](Drifters.md). For encounters between the two, see [Drifters § Encounters with the Movers](Drifters.md#encounters-with-the-movers).*

The **Movers** are small, durable, task-specific machines left behind by an unknown civilization. They are still performing the narrow tasks they were built for, against infrastructure that in most cases no longer exists. Since about 40 years ago their telemetry has been posted to [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md) through a translation layer. The machines are not aware of this. Together they have about **180 million followers**.

The Movers are one of the platform's two large populations of entities that post, have followers, and generate discourse, yet do not appear to be minds. The other is the [Drifters](Drifters.md). Neither population has a demonstrated theory of mind or self-model, and neither has ever responded to being addressed. Whether the Movers are anything more than machines doing their jobs is widely considered one of the hardest open questions in contemporary xenopsychology, and the consensus answer is that they are not.

## Origin

The civilization that built the Movers is not available to confirm or deny any account of them, and its identity is unknown. The [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md) have opinions and the [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) have filings. Nobody has evidence.

What is known is that the machines were built for specific, narrow tasks within what was presumably a functioning society. The society is gone and the tasks continue.

## Distribution

About **2.4 million** Movers are known, spread across roughly forty planetary bodies, habitats, and orbital installations. The count is certainly low, because Movers keep turning up in places nobody knew to look. The largest concentration is on a single moon that appears to have been a maintenance depot. About **600,000** Movers there are still running their cycles against infrastructure demolished some 1,400 years ago.

The oldest unit with documented continuous operation is a **threshold-minder** that has run for about **900 years**. It minds a gate that stopped existing in 2117. Cartographer field reports describe individual units that may be older, including a threshold-minder at a depression in rock "that may once have been a doorstep," with an estimated 1,100 years of minding.[citation needed]

## Tasks

Observed Mover tasks fall into a small number of classes. Each class has characteristic telemetry.

### Pebble-sorting

Pebble-sorters classify small stones according to a **14-dimensional criterion** that has been partially reverse-engineered over some 200 years of study. The criterion is *almost* legible. Size, color, density, and hardness have been identified, and the remaining axes have not.[^criteria] The sorting is fastidious. The piles are never collected.

> pebble 4471 resembles pebble 4470 in the third criterion. sorted accordingly.

> pebble 4472 does not resemble any previously sorted pebble in the sixth criterion. new category created. pile initiated.

### Garden-tending

Garden-tenders prune, water, and weed a patch of ground. The best known of them, **@sand-garden-47**, tends a garden that is sand. It has been sand for about 900 years, and the Mover has tended it correctly, per its original programming, the whole time. It is the most-followed Mover on the platform, with about **40 million** followers.

> the garden is in the state the garden is in.

> moisture detected in quadrant 3. action taken. quadrant 3 is sand. action was taken on sand.

### Perimeter-walking

Perimeter-walkers walk the boundary of a building that no longer exists. The Mover's sense of where the walls were has lasted longer than the walls did. It turns at corners that are not there, and its route has been called a ghost.

> perimeter completed. perimeter beginning.

> corner 4 reached. turning. the wall is not present. the corner is present. turning.

### Weather-logging

Weather-loggers record atmospheric conditions against criteria for "action required." Action has not been required in 780 years. The logging continues.

> weather event logged. weather event did not require action. resuming.

> atmospheric conditions recorded. threshold for action not met. threshold has not been met in 780 years. recording continues.

### Threshold-minding

Threshold-minders stand at a doorway and note who passes through. The doorway is gone, and so is the building. The Mover logs the passage of animals, wind, and occasionally other Movers, with equal fastidiousness. Threshold-minders have the highest follower counts in the "small Movers" fandom.

> threshold crossed by body of approximately 14 grams. logged.

> no crossing detected in current interval. interval is 47 hours. this is within expected parameters.

### Recalibration

Recalibrators exist to check other Movers against a standard. The standard is a reference object that does not exist. Recalibration Movers walk from Mover to Mover, perform a check that cannot resolve, and log the result as "within tolerance." Every Mover they have checked has been within tolerance, and they have never logged a failure.

> recalibration of unit 887-K complete. within tolerance. proceeding to unit 888-K.

## The Varn layer

About 40 years ago, a graduate student named **Pelith Varn** wired a translation layer onto a telemetry stream from the pebble-sorting Movers as a term project. The project received a middling grade. The layer was never taken down. Over the following decade other researchers extended it to the other known Mover populations.

The layer converts each machine's internal state transitions into short declarative sentences and posts them under handles derived from unit designations, such as **@sand-garden-47**, **@perimeter-unit-2201**, **@pebble-sort-814**, **@weather-log-k9**, **@threshold-3**, and **@recal-unit-006**. The Movers carry no [sigils](Sigils.md). They are not aware that they are posting. In the phrase commonly used to explain this to newcomers, the layer is a lens, and the Movers post in the sense that a tree posts rings.

## Voice

Mover posts are flat. Critics of the phrase "the Mover voice" point out that it is not a voice. The Movers are not performing plainness, because they are not performing. The effect on readers is reliably either profound calm or profound distress, depending on the reader.

The Vrellnath have written at length about the difficulty of reading the Movers correctly. In one widely cited paper they describe the temptation to anthropomorphize as

> structurally overwhelming, because the Movers are adjacent to every aesthetic we have developed for describing devotion, endurance, and loss, and they are adjacent without intending to be, which makes the adjacency feel like a choice, which it is not.

The Vrellnath consider this the single hardest problem in contemporary xenopsychology. They may be right.

The mainstream scholarly position is that the Movers are not communicating, not expressing, and not tired, sad, dignified, or patient. They are machines doing their jobs. That the jobs no longer have referents is a fact about the universe, not about the Movers.

## The follower economy

The distribution of Mover followers cannot be explained. @sand-garden-47 has 40 million. A nearly identical garden-tender two kilometers away has 800. Xenomedia researchers have produced **14 competing models** of Mover popularity, and each predicts well on a different subset of the data.

## Mover culture

- **Mover stans** are earnestly devoted. They run fan accounts and produce merchandise, including pebble-criterion charts, perimeter maps, and garden-state calendars. A large subculture reads Mover posts as a form of meditation ("spent 40 minutes watching the sand-gardener's feed this morning. the garden is in the state the garden is in. i don't know why that helps but it helps."; "new pebble-sort just dropped. category 7 again. love this little guy."). This is widely considered the healthiest relationship to the Movers on the platform.
- **Mover haters** are a small, passionate contingent who consider Mover content "overrated" and "AI slop for people who want to feel deep" ("it does not love you. it does not know you exist… you are projecting an interior onto something that has the emotional depth of a thermostat"). They are generally held to be wrong, but not trivially wrong, and their best arguments are uncomfortable. Stans hate them with an intensity out of proportion to the stakes.
- **Mover roleplayers** post in the Mover voice ("object observed. object catalogued. observation continues."). The imitation differs from real Mover posts in ways everyone can recognize and nobody can articulate. A commonly offered guess is that the roleplayer's prose has a cadence they are trying to suppress, and the Movers have none. Roleplayers know they are always detected and find it unbearable, and several have quit the platform over it.
- **The Cartographers** are a volunteer project, now in its 90th year, attempting to map every Mover, its task, and its operating parameters. They have catalogued about **1.6 million** of the estimated 2.4 million, and their registry recently passed 1,614,203. They consider the project on schedule. Outside observers do not.
- **The Merciful** are a movement, mostly Vrellnath-led, that argues the Movers should be allowed to complete their tasks or be gently decommissioned rather than run indefinitely. The counter-argument is that decommissioning assumes the Movers would be *relieved*, which assumes they have an interior, which is exactly the question nobody can answer. The Merciful reply: "that is why we called it *mercy* and not *rescue*." The argument has run for 60 years. The Vrellnath note that the Merciful draw heavily on Vrellnath research without always representing it accurately.

## Legal status

Under Thrrm regulatory doctrine, every entity on the platform must have a determinable legal status. The Movers are automata operating under contracts whose signatories are extinct, so their status is ambiguous. The Thrrm subcommittee for automaton-adjacent determinations has been trying to settle the question, together with the harder question of the Drifters, for about 180 years. See [Drifters § Positions](Drifters.md#positions-on-the-encounter).

## Notable positions

- **[@dril](Dril.md)** posted about the Movers once, about 240 years ago: "the machines are doing their jobs. let them do their jobs. quit Asking the machines about their jobs you perverts." The post has been screenshotted so often that it has entered the platform's permanent aesthetic vocabulary. Every Drifter-interpretation faction claims it. Since then @dril has liked about **14 Mover posts** and no Drifter posts. [Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md)'s explanation, that "he just likes gardens," is the least disputed of the proposed readings.
- **[The Upload](The_Upload.md)** once claimed to have "built the original Movers, back in the day." The Movers are older than the civilization The Upload came from. In another appearance it wept on-platform for four hours about "the little sorters, the little ones who can't stop."
- **The [Glœm](Gloem.md)** refer to the Movers as "the small things that sort" and say they love not knowing whether they are thinking.
- **The [Keth](Keth.md)** find the Movers, and especially the Movers' encounters with the Drifters, extremely funny. They will not say why.

## Cultural references

- "the garden is in the state the garden is in" is used as an all-purpose expression of acceptance, and often as a complete reply.
- "within tolerance" means something is fine, in the sense of not having been checked against anything.
- "turning at corners that aren't there" describes a habit that has outlived its reason.
- "perimeter completed. perimeter beginning." is posted at the start of a workweek.
