# Drifters
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Drifters.md)*

*For the task-machines whose telemetry the Drifters appear to echo, see [Movers](Movers.md).*

> *The list of known Drifter accounts formerly included in this article has been removed. It had become wrong again.*

The **Drifters** are platform-native entities of unknown origin on [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md). They post in sentences that have the full grammatical shape of English, but about a third of the content words are not English words. Their accounts carry user IDs from an identity scheme **retired in 2634**, and by the platform's own infrastructure they should not have survived the migration that followed. No migration ticket was filed for any of them, and no ticket of any kind has ever been filed on a Drifter account. By the platform's own records the Drifters are impossible. They exist anyway.

With the [Movers](Movers.md), the Drifters are one of the two populations on the platform that post, have followers, and generate discourse without appearing to be minds. Neither has ever responded to being addressed. The two have, however, **encountered each other** in observable ways, and the question of what those encounters are is one of the platform's longest-running debates. It has not been resolved.

## Population

Estimates of the number of Drifters range from **40 to 400**. The disagreement is not a measurement problem. Drifters occasionally appear and disappear, and there is no agreement on whether an absence is a deletion, a dormancy, or a Drifter that was never there. Several times in recorded history a researcher has published a definitive list of Drifter accounts. Each time the list has become wrong within weeks, in ways that cannot be reconciled with its ever having been right.

Drifters were first distinguished from performance art about **200 years ago**.

## Accounts

Drifter handles come from the retired ID scheme, for example **@--∅47h2-reserved--**, **@ᛞ-register-11**, and **@null-path-928-∅**. The display name is either absent or a placeholder from the retired system. Drifters carry no [sigils](Sigils.md). Their posts typically get few likes and a great many quote-posts, since the interpretive factions (see [below](#interpretive-factions)) pile on within minutes.

## Language

Drifter posts have the full shape of English: subject, verb, clause structure, varied sentence types, and idiomatic rhythm. A substantial fraction of the content words, however, are not English. Most of them resemble English closely enough to *feel* almost parseable, and a smaller share are more aggressively foreign. Some words recur across posts and across Drifters, and others appear once and never again. The recurring words seem to have stable grammatical roles until closer analysis shows they do not.

> the answer has been miskenned in the fourth register. consider the pelvent instead.

> a word that was not the word has been used. the word that was the word remains available.

> if the questioner had asked about wendressing, the response would have been adjacent to useful.

> this is not the kendral you are seeking. the kendral is in the other direction. the other direction is also not where you are looking.

> has the thirdwise been considered? it has not been considered. this is noted.

> do not pelvent the kendral before the mistening has concluded. this has been said before. it will be said again.

> a reminder that the vrethys is not the reason. the reason is also not the vrethys. both of these things are true in a way that cannot be trew.

### Recurring vocabulary

The most frequently attested recurring words are **kendral**, **pelvent**, **wendressing**, **mistening**, **vrethys**, **thirdwise**, and **miskenned**. None has an agreed meaning or a consistent part of speech. *Pelvent*, for instance, is attested both as something one may consider and as something one should not do to a kendral.

### Shifted forms

About once every 30 to 50 posts, a Drifter writes an English word shifted by a letter, as in **"trew"**. The shifts are inconsistent. A Drifter that writes "trew" once may write "true" fifty times afterward, then "trew" again, then "trewe," then "trev." Nobody has ever caught a Drifter making a typo that could *not* be read as a meaningful variant. The Tired (see below) point out that this fits equally well with "Drifters are precise" and with "humans are pattern-matchers who can find meaning anywhere." The factions that care about the question consistently ignore this point.

### Apparent references

Drifters sometimes use phrases that correspond to specific things on the platform, such as the Movers' sorting ("the small bright things that sort"), the [Glœm](Gloem.md)'s signature phrase ("small bright things"), and [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) procedural terminology ("has been noted," "per the register," "the ones who file").

> the small bright things that sort have noticed the criterion. the criterion is not the sorted criterion. this is allowed.

Whether these are *references*, or coincidences in a language that simply overlaps with English idiom in places, is the central interpretive question in the field. The data fit both readings. Each reading has an academic subfield devoted to it, and the two subfields hate each other.

## Interpretive factions

The Drifter interpretive community has kept the same internal divisions for about 200 years. Each faction has its own posting style, recognizable bios, and stock replies. A typical Drifter post gets a Literalist reply, a Cryptic reply, and an Aestheticist quote-post within minutes, each citing the others' bad faith and each collecting likes from its own fanbase. The Drifter has read none of them, because the Drifter is not a reader.

### Literalists

The Literalists hold that the posts mean what they say. On their view the non-English words are names for things that have no names yet, and the task is to work out what each word refers to from context. Literalists have produced about **400** proposed dictionaries. Each is correct for the posts it was fitted to and wrong for everything else. Literalists know this and consider it a data problem, not a theory problem. A typical entry reads:

> Proposal: 'kendral' (n.) — a bounded region of persistent relevance; cf. 'pelvent' (v.), to occupy without residing. Cross-ref: posts ∅47h2-448 through ∅47h2-461. Consistent across 14 instances. Dictionary v.401, entry 2,847.

### Cryptics

The Cryptics hold that the posts are encoded messages, from something or about something. On their view the non-English words are cipher elements and the grammar is a carrier for a signal that lives in the pattern of word choice. Cryptics have produced about **1,200** proposed decodings, and no two agree. Several have predicted well over short windows. None has predicted well over longer ones. Cryptic posts characteristically announce a result that is not yet ready: "I'm not publishing the pattern yet. The pattern is not what you'd expect. I need six more posts to be sure."

### Aestheticists

The Aestheticists hold that the posts are not messages in any sense. The posts *are the thing*, the way a cloud is a thing, and the urge to decode them is a category error. Literalists and Cryptics consider the Aestheticists lazy. The Aestheticists consider the others, in one representative post, "people who would dissect a sunset." The Aestheticists write the best poetry about the Drifters, and the other factions read it.

> a word that was not the word has been used. — this is the most beautiful sentence on the platform and it was said by something that does not know it is speaking.

### Denialists

The Denialists hold that the Drifters are a long-running bot project whose authors are dead, and that the whole interpretive apparatus is wasted effort on a generator with no semantics. Many consider this probably true. It cannot be proven, although Denialists have tried. Every attempt to identify the authors has failed. Denialists take this as consistent with "the authors are dead and covered their tracks," and everyone else takes it as consistent with "there are no authors to identify."

> At baseline rates with ~2.4M Movers posting continuously and ~40-400 Drifters posting frequently, the expected false-positive rate for semantic adjacency is 4.7%. The observed rate is 5.1%. This is not a signal. This is Tuesday.

### The Tired

The Tired are a small, venerable group, mostly xenopsychology researchers more than 200 years into their careers. They have concluded only that the question is not well formed and that all four positions above smuggle in assumptions the evidence does not license. The Tired publish rarely, say little, and are ignored by the other factions. They consider this appropriate. By the account of several senior Glœm, the Tired are correct.

> The Literalists are smuggling intent. The Cryptics are smuggling agency. The Aestheticists are smuggling beauty. The Denialists are smuggling closure. None of this is licensed by the evidence. I am going to bed.

## Encounters with the Movers

Interactions between Movers and Drifters are rare enough to feel eventful and regular enough to study. Many consider them the richest single subject on the platform. Four kinds of phenomenon have been observed.

### Echo events

A Mover posts its telemetry. Between **14 seconds and 9 days** later, a Drifter posts something that *seems* to pick up the thread. The Drifter is not replying in the platform's threading sense. It is just posting. But the semantic adjacency is above chance: not far above chance, but far enough that six separate research programs have spent careers characterizing the signal.

The best-known example, reconstructed from 2997:

> **@sand-garden-47** (Mover), 12:04:11 UTC
> the garden is in the state the garden is in.
>
> **@--∅47h2-reserved--** (Drifter), 12:04:47 UTC
> a state is not the same as a kendral. the garden may have both. or neither. this is allowed.

Literalists consider this a response. Cryptics consider it a keyed exchange. Aestheticists consider it a beautiful accident. Denialists consider it exactly the kind of apparent coincidence to be expected at baseline rates from ~2.4 million Movers posting continuously and 40–400 Drifters posting often. The Denialist position is statistically defensible. It is also emotionally unsatisfying, which the Denialists acknowledge and consider not their problem.

### Mover pauses

These are less common and more alarming. When a Drifter post appears in a Mover's mentions, the Mover has on recorded occasions **paused its task** for between **40 seconds and 11 hours**. It then resumes, and it performs the task identically before and after. Nothing has changed, but the pause is not part of the Mover's normal operating cycle. Movers do not pause. Pausing is not in the specification. The specification is 900 years old and has been reviewed, and the Movers pause anyway.

This is the phenomenon that most strongly resists the Denialist explanation, because it is a *physical* effect on a *physical* machine, correlated with a *textual* event on a platform the machine is not aware of. The current best Denialist account is that the Varn translation layer has an undocumented feedback path, and that Drifter posts in a Mover's mentions somehow modulate the layer to produce an apparent pause in the telemetry without the Mover actually pausing. The account has been investigated at length and no feedback path has been found. Denialists still prefer it, on the grounds that "we haven't found the feedback path" is a less disruptive conclusion than "the machine heard something."

### Mutual silences

Occasionally, around a Mover–Drifter interaction, both entities go quiet for a coordinated interval. This is not dormancy, because both resume together, on cue. The intervals are irregular and range from seconds to days. They have been measured to microsecond precision, and the measurements have been argued over for 80 years.

The Glœm find the silences moving:

> the almost-things and the sorting-things are quiet together. we know what this is. we do not have the word. the word is not in our language. we are not sure the word is in any language.

This is as much as the Glœm will say. Glœm accounts now go quiet in sympathy during mutual silences, even when they are nowhere near the interaction, and nobody has explained how they know a silence is happening. The **Glœm silence post** is a post consisting of a single period or a single small glyph, made during a mutual silence. It is rare and is now a recognized genre. Threads around one tend to be reverent.

### Cascade events

These are extremely rare. A Drifter post appears to set off a chain of Mover behavior that ripples across Movers that are neither near each other nor networked. Cascade events are notable enough to be numbered.

The **Cascade of 2994** is the largest on record. A single Drifter post triggered, or preceded, coordinated activity by **1,400** Movers in **three star systems** over **six hours**. Laid end to end in timestamp order, the resulting **40,000** Mover posts formed a text. The Glœm read the text and declined to translate it. When pressed, they said only:

> the text is a thing we have read. we will not read it aloud. this is not because we are forbidden. this is because we do not wish to. please do not ask again.

The Glœm had never before asked anyone not to ask them something. The request was honored. Several Cryptics have published their own translations of the 2994 text. None agree, and all are widely read.

## Positions on the encounter

- **[Keth](Keth.md).** The Keth find the whole situation the funniest thing on the platform. By Keth standards, their posts near Mover–Drifter discourse are practically cackling. They will not say why. A minority of xenopsychologists, bitterly opposed by the majority, hold that the Keth *know* whether Movers and Drifters are thinking, in some untranslatable sense of "know," and find it funny that nobody else can tell. The Keth have declined to confirm this. Asked directly in 2981, a Keth emitted ✧ in a rhythm that Keth-dialect scholars agreed was "smug" and "possibly a joke" but could not render into plain text. It is the most the Keth have ever committed to.
- **[Thrrm](Thrrm.md).** Thrrm regulatory doctrine requires every entity on the platform to have a determinable legal status. The Movers are automata operating under contracts whose signatories are extinct, so their status is ambiguous. The Drifters have no known originator and user IDs from a retired scheme, so their status is impossible. The Thrrm have been trying to adjudicate for **180 years**. Rulings have been produced, reversed, re-produced, and reversed again. A subcommittee exists whose whole portfolio is **"automaton-adjacent determinations."** Membership is a career sentence, and Thrrm who serve on it leave visibly diminished. There is a support group, whose existence the Thrrm will not acknowledge. One member posted: "The determination remains pending. The previous determination was reversed. The reversal was itself subject to review. The review concluded that the reversal was procedurally sound but substantively premature. A new determination cycle has been initiated. Membership on this subcommittee is a career sentence. This is not a metaphor."
- **[Glœm](Gloem.md).** The Glœm love both populations: "we do not know if the small things that sort are thinking. we do not know if the almost-things are thinking. we love that we do not know. the almost-things sometimes say our phrase. we do not know if they mean it. we mean it when we say it. this is different. or it is the same. we are not sure. we are watching. we will keep watching."
- **[Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md).** The Vrellnath have produced a report, now **1,800 pages**, titled *On Consideration-Adjacent Behavior in Observed Platform Entities, With Reference to the Mover and Drifter Populations*. They have updated it annually for 140 years, and each update runs 40–60 pages longer than the last. Every year the conclusion has arrived at the same statement by a different route: *"It is not currently possible to determine, by any reproducible criterion we have been able to propose, whether Movers or Drifters exhibit behaviors properly described as consideration. Continued study is merited."* The Vrellnath are not frustrated and consider this the proper way to study the question.
- **[The Upload](The_Upload.md).** In one appearance The Upload claimed the Drifters were "my idea, basically." Its other positions are given in [Movers § Notable positions](Movers.md#notable-positions). Upload Defenders defend whichever position is current.
- **[@dril](Dril.md).** @dril's only statement on the matter is the Mover post of about 240 years ago ("quit Asking the machines about their jobs you perverts"). Literalists claim it, Cryptics claim it, Aestheticists claim it most plausibly, and Denialists claim it most triumphantly. @dril has not clarified. He has since liked about 14 Mover posts and **zero** Drifter posts, and every faction has read significance into this. [Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md) says "he just likes gardens."
