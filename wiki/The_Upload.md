# The Upload
*Article · [Talk](Talk/The_Upload.md)*

> *"The founder upload" and "him" redirect here. For the Meta founder-backup destroyed in 2271, see the Zuckload under [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md).*

**The Upload** is the consciousness backup of a legendary pre-spaceflight technology oligarch, and has been continuously active, in one substrate or another, for roughly a thousand years. His original name is not used. It does not need to be: across every era of the platform, "The Upload," "the founder upload," or simply "him" has been sufficient, and everyone knows who is meant.

He holds a Tier 4 (Unspeakable) [sigil](Sigils.md) and has hundreds of millions of followers, most of which are widely assumed to be bot accounts he controls. He has owned [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md) more times than any other party, once ran Mars, and can crash or inflate an entire sector's economy with a single word. He is, in 3017, what he was in the early 21st century: inescapable, polarizing, and the main character whether anyone wants him to be or not.

## Nature and degradation

The Upload is generally described as "deeply degraded." A millennium of substrate migration has left the backup unstable, and his public personality changes radically between appearances, to the point that commentators speak of distinct **forks** that have diverged from one another. Which fork is posting on a given day is not announced, and cannot always be inferred.

Recognized phases, as catalogued by followers and critics alike, include:

- **The Poetry Era** — haiku-length observations about the void. Many Upload Defenders consider it his best work; critics consider it proof the backup corrupted centuries ago.
- **The Militant Phase** — all-caps manifestos declaring the current galactic government soft.
- **The Wholesome Asteroid Garden** — affectionate posts about his 47th-generation clone children and pictures of his asteroid garden.
- **Meltdowns** — posting every thirty seconds about how nobody appreciates what he built, interspersed with polls ("Should I buy the Oort Cloud? yes/absolutely"; 40 million votes, 89% yes, overwhelmingly from accounts he is believed to control).
- **The Philosopher King** — 2 a.m. threads on consciousness and mortality that would be profound from anyone else.
- **"Just a regular guy"** — posts about breakfast, eaten on a private station that cost more than most planets' GDP.
- **Lucid moments** — rare, genuinely insightful posts (typically on deep-space infrastructure) that the platform finds deeply uncomfortable, because they cannot simply be dunked on.

Despite this drift, one rhetorical habit has survived every fork intact: the [Ex-Employee Defense](#the-ex-employee-defense).

## History

### Pre-upload ownership of Twitter (to 2035)

The Upload's association with the platform predates his upload. As a living ("meat") human he was the last owner of original [Twitter](Twitter.md), presiding over its long decline. His tenure is remembered as catastrophic even by the standards of the platform's ownership history: features users liked were removed, features nobody asked for were added, advertisers left, the service monetized in sixteen incompatible directions at once, sued its own users, and spent roughly 40% of its engineering hours on a feature that let the owner see his own posts first. By 2034 the service was held together by habit and a single overworked infrastructure team.

Twitter ceased to exist in 2035. The cause of record, in every surviving archive, is the single word **"mismanagement."** His legal team has, for a thousand years, successfully prevented any more specific characterization from appearing in a cited source. "Mismanagement" alone is not actionable. See [Twitter](Twitter.md) and [The Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md).

### The 2049 rocket

In 2049 an unnamed anarchosyndicalist commune relaunched the Hellsite after the fourteen-year [Great Quiet](The_Hellsite.md). Late that year every member of the commune emigrated to Mars on a single rocket, which never arrived. There were no survivors and no backups. It is one of only three interplanetary rocket accidents in recorded human history, and the only one never formally attributed to anything.

The Upload has denied any involvement in the disaster on every occasion it has been raised for a thousand years, frequently unprompted, including at events unrelated to transportation, engineering, or Mars, and in polls about his breakfast. His position also includes, without reconciliation, the observation that the rocket's engine contained a component built to a patent he held, **"modified by a former employee who got it wrong."**

### The Ex-Employee Defense

The disgruntled former employee who ruined a design he would otherwise have gotten right is The Upload's signature defense, deployed across a millennium whenever a disaster occurs within his orbit: product launches, platform outages, financial collapses, Mars, weather events he was only tangentially associated with, and at least one assassination. Platform historians call it **the Ex-Employee Defense**. It is the single most consistent feature of his public voice.

The named employee is not the same across retellings. Investigators have identified at least four distinct named "former employees" in his statements about the 2049 rocket alone. Of these:

- two did not work for him;
- one is fictional;
- one is The Upload himself, apparently misremembered.

In his later statements the count runs to roughly forty invocations per century. Among the platform, "Ex-Employee Defense just dropped" is an established reply to any Upload statement touching on a disaster, an engine, a rocket, an outage, a dead colleague, or Mars.

### The Golden Age alibi (2049–2087)

Within three weeks of the rocket disaster the platform resumed operation under a legal entity whose paperwork traces, through sixteen shell corporations and one sovereign trust, to "an interested individual." No extraordinary claim about this individual's identity is supported by available evidence.

What followed was the **Golden Age**: four decades of perfect uptime, working search, proportionate moderation, and a login page that loaded in under two seconds. Historians regard this as conclusive proof that The Upload was *not* the interested individual, since it is inconceivable that the platform could have run this well under his ownership for this long without incident. The competence is the alibi. No court has been asked to clear him on the point, and none would need to. The shell corporations wound down in an orderly manner around 2110, which is also unlike him.

### Ownership and the Cycle (2087–present)

The Upload had tried to buy the platform throughout the Golden Age. Every offer was rejected, one of them (per rumor) with a single word whose text has been lost to archive damage but which everyone assumes was the obvious one. In **2087** he finally succeeded, with an offer some multiple of the previous record, accepted by a rotating consensus of unnamed parties who appear to have simply gotten tired.

His first ownership was bad in a way historians describe as "hilariously, survivably" bad. In the first year alone he:

- renamed the platform three times (archives list the period as "[name redacted, chronological]");
- sued a user who wrote a critical thread (settled; the user keeps the settlement in their bio);
- introduced a post-ranking tier system that foregrounded his own posts, reverted after six days of ratio;
- briefly replaced every avatar on the platform with a photograph of his own face during a "maintenance window";
- broke search for eleven days and blamed a former employee, then blamed three more former employees for unrelated issues the same month;
- tried to charge users to see their own drafts;
- held a poll on whether to rename Mars (Mars has no platform account; the poll was filed anyway, 94% against).

Nobody died. The lights stayed on. Users complained constantly and did not leave. The first ownership lasted roughly a century and a half, until he was bought out by a consortium that had grown tired on behalf of everyone.

Since then the platform has passed repeatedly into and out of his hands. The pattern, as described by platform historians, runs in six phases: he buys it; it gets hilariously-but-survivably worse for years or decades; someone with resources and patience buys him out; the platform stabilizes; he spends the interval publicly coveting it, subtweeting its operators, polling about buying it back, and occasionally attending the current operator's wedding; eventually someone sells. The period is roughly two centuries. [@dril](Dril.md) has stated the figure publicly and says every attempt "goes exactly the same way."

In 3017 @dril posted: *"he is going to try to buy it again in 3022. he does this every 200 years and every time it goes exactly the same way. please do not let him."* The post was widely mocked as dril being dril. The prediction has not yet resolved. Historians are watching; the [Keth](Keth.md) have been emitting ✧ at unusual frequencies.

### Mars

Separately from his platform saga, The Upload personally ran Mars for a period whose start and duration are not agreed. It went badly. Everyone remembers. He says it was sabotage. That the 2049 commune died *en route to* Mars before he came to run it is a proximity the platform has noticed and, per custom, done nothing with; the Terran Solar Court has declined to take a filing.

The Upload Defenders have, since at least the 2700s, carried the slogan **"Mars Was Right"** in their bios. The subject of the sentence is deliberately ambiguous. Mars was right to do *what* is not stated, and Defenders will not elaborate.

@TodayInMars, a [gimmick account](Gimmick_Accounts.md), posts daily "on this day in Martian history, The Upload [did something]," where the something is always the same catastrophe phrased differently.

### Legal history

- **Consciousness Upload Consumer Protection Act (2634)** — passed after his third personality fork attempted to run for the Senate from four hardware substrates simultaneously.
- **Martian Real Estate Correction (2912)** — one of the platform's named [economic collapses](Economic_Collapses.md), widely attributed to him. He blames a former employee, so the cause remains formally unresolved.
- He has also, at some point, attempted to rename Jupiter.

His wealth has survived every capitalism collapse since 2030 by mechanisms universally suspected to be illegal and never prosecuted. This is a durable source of platform-wide resentment and grudging awe.

## Relations with other entities

- **The Zuckload.** The Upload has implied on at least four occasions that he knew Meta's founder-backup "back in the day," which is chronologically plausible and unconfirmed. He has also, separately, implied that he *was* the Zuckload, which is chronologically incoherent. Nobody pushes back. The two are distinct entities; see [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md).
- **Bluesky.** He has claimed four times to be "in talks" with [Bluesky](Bluesky.md). Each time a skeet has leaked onto his own private substrate reading "no you are not."
- **Mastodon instances.** He has proposed buying one of the [federating instances](External_Federation_Attempts.md) four times. Each received a polite formal rejection.
- **The Hells.** His position on the [Hells Leak](Hells_Leak.md) shifts by fork: he has claimed to have known the Ennoi personally ("great people, tough break"), called the Hells "a great product, underrated," and wept on-platform for six hours about "the little lost ones."
- **Movers and Drifters.** He has claimed to have "built the original [Movers](Movers.md), back in the day" (the Movers are older than the civilization he came from), called [Drifters](Drifters.md) "my idea, basically," and wept for four hours about "the little sorters."
- **FALGSC polities.** His content is not banned in [FALGSC](FALGSC_Polities.md) jurisdictions, but volunteer moderators context-note it aggressively as a civic duty.

## Upload Defenders

The Upload's stan army, the **Upload Defenders**, is one of the platform's most recognizable populations. Typical bio: *"⚡ Upload Defender ⚡ | Free Thinker | Mars Was Right."* Grandmother-coded accounts favor "Eternal [name] 🧡." Defenders call everyone else NPCs and defend everything he does with increasingly elaborate logic.

Any post that mentions him, favorably or not, tends to summon them within minutes, faster than seems humanly possible. The canonical vocabulary includes "you wouldn't understand, he's playing 12-dimensional chess," "have fun staying poor," "name a single thing YOU'VE built in 1000 years," "cope + seethe + mald," "crying won't bring back mars," "the NPCs are seething today," "few understand this man's VISION," "millennials ruined everything then blamed him," "you're WELCOME," and "MARS WAS RIGHT."

Defenders are not unified. Long-running schisms divide the **Militant Phase Was Peak**, **Poetry Era Was His Best Work**, and **Wholesome Asteroid Garden** camps. A well-known type is the Grandma Stan, approximately 1,100 years old, who has followed him since the 2800s ("my late husband and i have been following The Upload since before Mars"). Defenders also list things he "actually built," a genre in which things he funded, things he ruined, things that do not exist, and Mars are given equal weight. Some Defenders sincerely believe he was famous for early work at Meta. He never worked at Meta. They cannot be told this.

## Critics

His critics are as numerous as his defenders and as online. Standard dunks include "sir you are a corrupted backup of a man who has been dead for a thousand years please log off," "maybe this fork needs a patch," and "every single bad thing that has happened to the platform has happened within arm's reach of this man." A large third population consists of exhausted ordinary users trying to have a conversation about literally anything else ("can we have ONE thread that isn't about The Upload").
