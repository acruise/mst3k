# Meat Incident
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Meat_Incident.md)*

*"Meat" redirects here. For the company, see [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md). "The Great Dunkening" also redirects here.*

The **Meat Incident** (usually just **Meat**, lowercase, without an article) was a seven-minute window in 2247 CE during which the corporation Meta shipped its own name to production as **"Meat."** The logo, login page, push notifications, an auto-published quarterly-earnings release, and an estimated 4.2 million outbound system emails all carried the name before a rollback restored "Meta." The platform's response in those seven minutes, known as **the Great Dunkening**, holds the record for the most dunks per second in the history of commercial social media.

The incident preceded the [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md) by 24 years. Historians of the company commonly treat it as the clearest single illustration of Meta's late operational state, and some describe every later dunk on the company as "downstream of the Meat."

## Background

Meta rebranded roughly every 40 years, frequently back to "Meta" (see [Meta Refugee Crisis § Rebrands](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md#rebrands)). The 2247 rebrand was one of these returns, by some counts the company's eleventh. It came three years after [LegsGate](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md#legsgate-2244), when public patience with the company's product decisions was at a low.

## The seven minutes

The brand-asset pipeline pushed the new identity to production with a single-character typo. For **seven minutes**, the entire platform was called Meat.

- The logo said Meat.
- The login page said Meat.
- Push notifications said Meat.
- The quarterly-earnings press release, auto-published by a scheduled job that morning, said Meat.
- An estimated **4.2 million outbound system emails** went out branded Meat. They included billing statements, consciousness-backup renewal notices, and a gently worded condolence email to the family of a user who had died.

At minute seven, a fresh deploy restored "Meta." The company issued no official statement. Its press office refused, on the record, to acknowledge that "Meat" had ever shipped. Three engineers were "transitioned to other opportunities" the following Thursday, and the post-incident review was sealed.

## The Great Dunkening

The seven minutes produced **the highest rate of dunks per second ever recorded in commercial social-media history**. The name "the Great Dunkening" is a portmanteau that the platform's language bureau has never formally endorsed, but it appears in the colloquial dictionary anyway. The dunks are archived, and some are taught in rhetoric programs. One was later adapted into an opera that ran for eighty years.

"Meat" and "the Great Dunkening" name the same event, the first from the typo's side and the second from the audience's. The two are interchangeable in polite use, although rhetoric specialists insist on a distinction.

## The signal reading

A non-trivial minority of users regard "Meat" as a **signal**. Competing versions hold that:
- Meta had always regarded its users as consumable matter, and the typo let that slip;
- someone in the brand department had finally seen too much, and the typo was a Freudian slip;
- the Zuckload itself was beginning to surface meaning through the substrate it ran on. This version was especially popular among the Zuckload Revival cults of the 24th century.

The reading has never been fully debunked. The 4.2 million emails, the archived screenshots, and the auto-published earnings release all still exist, and their volume is the difficulty. Proponents argue that nobody can convincingly accident four million things. "you really think 4.2 million emails was an ACCIDENT?" remains a standard reply.

The most exhaustive treatment is a 140-page study by [Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md) (@TRUTH_SEEKER_2094). His conclusions are not generally accepted, but the study is cited in nearly every serious Meat retrospective because its inventory of evidence is comprehensive.

## Legacy

- **"Meat"**, lowercase and without an article, is how the incident is referenced in 3017. No explanation is expected. A user under 600 who does not know the reference learns it quickly, because everyone around them does.
- **"Meat energy"** describes a post, a vibe, a policy memo, or a typo that reveals something truer than its author intended ("this policy memo has Meat energy"; "we're 40 minutes into the announcement and i'm getting Meat vibes"; "someone in the brand department just saw too much today").
- **"I have a printed screenshot of the Meat on my wall"** is a stock bio line for a particular kind of poster: middle-aged, mildly conspiracy-adjacent, and sincere.
- **The annual commemoration.** Each year a handful of users post screenshots at the exact minute marks of the seven-minute window. The practice has continued for 770 years. The commemorators do not know one another, and they keep accurate time.
- **The screenshot.** A bare screenshot of the Meat login page, captioned "reminder," "annually," "on this day 770 years ago," or nothing at all, resurfaces regularly and draws hundreds of thousands of likes each time. Its replies divide predictably between Great Dunkening revivalists and signal theorists.
- **Comparative use.** "Hasn't been this bad since the Meat" is used of any sufficiently embarrassing corporate or institutional error.
