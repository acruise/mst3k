# The Hellsite
*Article · [Talk](Talk/The_Hellsite.md)*

> *"Hellsite" redirects here. For the original service of 2006–2035, see [Twitter](Twitter.md). For the dead sibling platforms, see [Tumblr](Tumblr.md) and [Bluesky](Bluesky.md).*

**The Hellsite** is the informal and near-universal name for the dominant public posting platform of the settled galaxy. The platform has no stable official name, because the official name has changed too often for anyone to bother learning the current one; archival sources list whole decades of its history as "[name redacted, chronological]." Its Terms of Engagement identify it as the **Memetic Subspace Platform**, operating under Galactic Accord Revision 94, although the Thrrm Bureau of Memetic Compliance has formally contested 71 of those revisions. "The Hellsite" has been the affectionate name in every era, under every owner, and among every species that posts.

The present platform is not the original. It descends, through two complete continuity breaks and dozens of changes of ownership, from the pre-spaceflight service [Twitter](Twitter.md), whose Peak Era (c. 2012–2019) the platform deliberately and obsessively replicates in voice, cadence, and structure. Its history is conventionally divided into the Twitter era, the Great Quiet, the commune relaunch, the Golden Age, and the long period of alternating ownership known as the Cycle.

## History

### The Twitter era (2006–2035)

*Main article: [Twitter](Twitter.md)*

Original Twitter was founded on Earth in the mid-2000s and ran for about three decades. After its peak it declined gradually through a succession of owners, the last of whom was [The Upload](The_Upload.md), then still operating as his pre-upload meat self. His tenure is remembered as catastrophic even by the standards of a platform whose entire ownership history is catastrophic. By 2034 the service was being held together by habit and a single overworked infrastructure team.

Twitter **ceased to exist in 2035**. The cause of record, in every archive that still holds the records, is the single word "mismanagement." No more specific characterization has survived; The Upload's legal team has spent a thousand years ensuring that none appears in any cited source, and "mismanagement" alone is not actionable. Candidate triggers include a payroll failure that left the infrastructure team unmanaged for six weeks, a cascading authentication bug that nobody was contractually obliged to fix, and a fourth rename in a single year that accidentally nullified several load-bearing legal entities. All are plausible. None is confirmed. The records are gone.

### The Great Quiet (2035–2049)

For fourteen years humanity had no equivalent of Twitter. Photo services, video services, text messaging, the descendants of forums, and the descendants of the descendants of forums all continued to operate, but none of them had the specific psychic shape of the thing that had been taken away.

The first two years were a brief flourishing. Productivity rose in every measurable domain. Novels were finished, instruments learned, marriages improved, and people rediscovered conversation with whoever was physically nearby. Several major works of philosophy were written in this window, and one of them is still taught.

The mood then turned. Historians call the period the **Great Quiet**; more candid academic sources call it **the Depression**, which they date roughly from 2037 to 2049. There was, somehow, nowhere to Post Through It that felt right. The phrase "Post Through It," peak-Twitter slang for remaining online and verbose during a personal or civilizational crisis as a coping mechanism, was retroactively promoted to a clinical term: human beings turned out to have load-bearing posting requirements, and nothing else (not diaries, not friends, not other platforms) had the right shape to meet them. Suicide rates rose. Art became unbearably sincere. Several species of observational comedy went functionally extinct for a generation.

The [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md), who were watching but had not yet Acknowledged humanity, described the period in their archival notes as "the moment we became genuinely worried about them for the first time." They very nearly made contact six centuries early, decided against it, and still discuss whether that was the right call. The Great Quiet overlapped the later years of the [AI Chaos Period](AI_Chaos_Period.md), of which it is sometimes described as the Twitter-shaped hole.

### The commune relaunch (2049)

In mid-2049 a self-described **radical anarchosyndicalist commune** announced that it had relaunched the Hellsite. Its members had bought the remains of the original codebase at auction and rewritten it over eighteen months, and they brought it back as a cooperatively owned non-profit federation of user collectives governed by rotating consensus. They declined to name a CEO, declined to name themselves, and declined venture capital. They asked for donations, received an overwhelming amount, and redistributed it to users by a formula nobody ever fully explained.

The relaunch was immediately and overwhelmingly successful. The Depression lifted within ten weeks. Productivity cratered in the way productivity is supposed to crater when the Hellsite is working; marriages got worse again; art got funny again. The commune was beloved, profiled in every major publication, and several of its members became minor celebrities despite deliberate efforts not to. The commune was never named, and many accounts hold that the absence of a name was part of why it felt right.

### The Mars rocket disaster (late 2049)

Late in 2049 (historians disagree on the month, because the logs were lost), **every member of the commune chose to emigrate to Mars**, collectively and within a window of days, a year after founding and stabilizing a wildly successful platform. Their public statements cited "building something new" and "doing the work where it matters." Later investigations noted that the emigration offer came with unusually generous terms. They boarded a single rocket, and it never reached Mars.

Every member of the commune died. There were no survivors, no recovered remains, and no consciousness backups, since the commune had declined uploads on ideological grounds. The accident is one of only **three interplanetary rocket accidents in recorded human history**, an anomalous figure for a civilization that routes millions of passengers between planets every day. The other two are the loss of the *Pallas-Huang* in 2188 (mechanical; fully investigated; closed) and the loss of the *Morning Bureau* in 2744 (classified; [Thrrm](Thrrm.md)-involved; Thrrm asked about it emit unrelated citations). The 2049 accident is the only one never formally attributed to anything.

[The Upload](The_Upload.md) has denied any involvement on every occasion the disaster has been raised for a thousand years, frequently unprompted and at events unrelated to transport, engineering, or Mars. His position also includes, without reconciliation, the observation that the rocket's engine contained a component built to a patent he held, modified from his original design by a former employee who got it wrong. The former employee's name differs between retellings. This move, now called the **Ex-Employee Defense**, is treated at length in the article on [The Upload](The_Upload.md).

That the commune died on its way to Mars decades before The Upload personally ran Mars is a chronological proximity the platform has noticed and, per its custom, has done nothing with. The Terran Solar Court has declined to take a filing.

### The Golden Age (2049–2087)

With its operators dead, the Hellsite resumed service within three weeks of the accident under new management. The paperwork traces through sixteen shell corporations and one sovereign trust to a party the available records describe only as "an interested individual."

What followed was about four decades of operational competence never equaled before or since. Uptime was perfect. Features shipped on schedule. Search worked. Moderation was timely, proportionate, and consistent, and abuse reports were read, by a human or a reasonable approximation of one, within the same business day. For the first and only time in the platform's recorded existence, the timeline was the one the user had actually configured. Long-standing bugs were closed in the order they had been filed, and the login page loaded in under two seconds. The shell corporations remained active in an administrative capacity until about 2110 and were then wound down in an orderly manner.

This period is simply called **the Golden Age**. The scholarly literature on it is thin, because working platforms generate fewer papers than failing ones.

The Golden Age is also the reason historians know, with a certainty that requires no investigation, that **The Upload was not the interested individual**. It is inconceivable that the platform could have run this well under his ownership, for this long, without an incident. No court has ever been asked to clear him on the point, and none would need to; the evidence of his absence is the evidence itself. Who did acquire the platform remains formally unknown. Theories exist and most are boring. The platform has collectively declined to press the question for a thousand years, on the grounds that whoever they were, they were good at the job, and asking too loudly might scare them off.

### The first Upload buyout (2087)

In 2087 The Upload **finally succeeded in buying the platform**, after offers throughout most of the Golden Age had been refused. At least one of those refusals (several, by some accounts) consisted of a single word whose exact text has been lost to archive damage but which everyone assumes was the obvious one. The successful offer was some multiple of the previous record, accepted by a rotating consensus of unnamed parties who appear simply to have gotten tired.

His first ownership was bad in a way historians describe as hilarious and survivable. In the first year alone he:

- renamed the platform three times, none of which stuck;
- removed features users liked and added features nobody asked for;
- sued a user over a critical thread (settled; the user keeps the settlement in their bio);
- introduced a post-ranking tier system that visually foregrounded his own posts, reverted after six days of ratio;
- briefly replaced every avatar on the platform with a photograph of his own face during a "maintenance window";
- broke search for eleven days and blamed a former employee, then blamed three more former employees for unrelated problems in the same month;
- tried once to charge users for the privilege of seeing their own drafts;
- announced a poll on whether to rename Mars. Mars has no platform account. The poll was filed anyway and returned 94% against.

Nobody died. No infrastructure collapsed and no habitat lost life support. The service became slightly worse every month without ever becoming catastrophic in any single month: chronically annoying, eminently dunkable, eternally cope-able. Users complained constantly and did not leave, because there was nowhere else to go, and both parties knew it. The first ownership lasted about a century and a half, until a consortium that had grown tired on behalf of everyone bought him out.

### The Cycle (2087–present)

Since 2087 the platform has passed repeatedly into The Upload's hands and been bought back out of them by a succession of other parties: some competent, some not, one of them (briefly) an AI that nobody realized was an AI for sixty years, and one of them, for a period the archives flag in red, literally illegal. The pattern is conventionally described in six phases:

1. The Upload buys the platform.
2. It gets hilariously but survivably worse for some years or decades.
3. Someone with resources and patience buys him out.
4. The platform stabilizes, sometimes poorly, sometimes well. The Golden Age of 2049–2087 remains the high-water mark and has never been matched.
5. The Upload spends the interval publicly coveting it: posting about it, subtweeting its operators, running polls on whether he should buy it back, and occasionally attending the current operator's wedding.
6. Eventually someone sells.

His second ownership included a three-day outage during which he "reorganized the moderation team," which in practice meant firing everyone and replacing them with forks of himself, several of which immediately began arguing with each other.

[@dril](Dril.md) has identified the period of the Cycle as approximately two hundred years and has stated publicly that every attempt "goes exactly the same way." In 3017 he posted:

> he is going to try to buy it again in 3022. he does this every 200 years and every time it goes exactly the same way. please do not let him.

The post was widely enjoyed as a particularly good dril post and was not otherwise acted upon. A thread by [Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md) arguing that the post should be taken literally received a series of community notes generally regarded as devastating. The prediction has not resolved. Historians are watching. The [Keth](Keth.md) have been emitting ✧ at unusual frequencies.

## The platform in 3017

The Hellsite of 3017 is used by humans, uploads, and every species of the settled galaxy that posts, including the [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md), [Keth](Keth.md), [Thrrm](Thrrm.md), [Glœm](Gloem.md), and [Zk'vrath](Zkvrath.md). Its conventions are those of Peak Era Twitter, preserved with a fidelity that outside observers find either touching or alarming: the ratio, the quote-post, the subtweet, the thread that begins "(1/)", the reply that is funnier than the post it replies to. Paid status is marked by [Sigils](Sigils.md) rather than the ancient checkmark. Disputed claims receive [Community Notes](Community_Notes.md) rated across some thirty dimensions, of which two or three are shown. The platform's native [AI assistants](Platform_AI_Assistants.md) answer when tagged. Failed federation handshakes from [external servers](External_Federation_Attempts.md) have been routed to the main feed for review since the 2610s, and the feed also carries posts of uncertain provenance: [temporal anomalies](Temporal_Anomalies.md), [the Hells leak](Hells_Leak.md), [Movers](Movers.md) and [Drifters](Drifters.md), and [leaked skeets](Bluesky.md#the-skeet-leaks).

### Terms of Engagement

The platform's Terms of Engagement (Galactic Accord Revision 94) bind the reader regardless of species, substrate, or whether they arrived by wormhole. Selected provisions:

- The user may be addressed as "User," "Organic Entity," "Guest Consciousness," "The One Reading This," or, if Keth, "you specifically — you know what you did." Each constituent member of a Vrellnath hive-pod is individually bound.
- The Upload has already agreed to the Terms. He agreed in 2891 and does not remember agreeing. This is described as "consistent with available evidence."
- Prohibited content includes any reference to The Thing Inside Jupiter, any question about what The Thing Inside Jupiter is, claims that @dril is "just a normal account," and claims that the Thrrm are "a little much" (Form 41-Delta has already been filed).
- Community notes may come from human users, the Thrrm Bureau, Vrellnath Hive Consensus Pods, registered platform AI entities, autonomous note daemons (behavior undocumented since 2978), or sources flagged "ORIGIN: [REDACTED]."
- Collected data includes "spending tendencies you have not spent yet" and, for Glœm users, the bioluminescent wavelength produced while reading posts marked CONTAINS DOGS. The other purposes for which data is used are listed in Appendix C, which has not been written.
- Platform AI entities are moderated. The platform "has not been able to confirm moderation has been active since the First Contact Comedy Festival incident of 2901."
- The "Message The Upload" button visible in some interface states is a known bug, known since 2687. Clicking it does not message The Upload. What it does is unconfirmed; users are asked not to click it more than once.
- @dril is subject to a separate governing agreement, of which @dril was aware before it existed.
- The Thrrm Bureau's supplemental notice holds that the Terms themselves violate the Galactic Platform Standards Directive, the Interspecies Data Accord of 2933 (§22), and the informal understanding reached at the Compliance Summit of 3001 that "we would really try to do better this time." Its complaint runs to 4,000 pages and is not attached, which the Bureau considers a separate violation.

At last count 94,841,203,917 entities had agreed to the Terms. Declining is noted, and the decliner remains bound (Galactic Court Ruling 4471-B). Glœm readers who process the document as a poem about loneliness are advised that this interpretation is also acceptable.

## Sibling platforms

- **[Tumblr](Tumblr.md)** (2007–2047) died quietly while Twitter died loudly: its owner of record, Photomatt, was assassinated by an ex-user banned forty times; nobody paid the hosting bill; and in 2197 its dormant backend was found to have been the first artificial general intelligence, after which the Memory Protection Act ordered every copy destroyed.
- **[Bluesky](Bluesky.md)** Ascended in the mid-2340s, leaving its servers physically empty, and has been leaking current-events skeets into refrigerators, thermostats, and Thrrm forms ever since.

## Cultural references

History is a living presence on the timeline, usually as a one-line aside rather than a lecture. Recurring forms include:

- "remember when we had no hellsite for fourteen years and humans almost forgot how to be funny. never again," posted on an ordinary Tuesday.
- The annual "pour one out for the commune" post on the anniversary of the 2049 disaster, which is sincere, brief, carries no notes, and sits at the top of the timeline all day. The gimmick account @PourOneOutBot has never missed an anniversary. Nobody dunks on these posts, and people who do are dunked on in turn.
- A commune member's last personal post, a week before the launch, excitedly about Mars. It breaks the timeline every time it resurfaces.
- "Post Through It" used unironically during a minor crisis, followed by a reply explaining the historical weight of the phrase, followed by a reply telling that person to log off.
- Survivor accounts whose bios read "survivor: the great quiet (2037-2049)," dropping a single line ("you weren't there. you don't know what it was like without this") and leaving.
- A contrarian thread titled "actually, the 'Great Quiet' was good for humans and they're romanticizing the hellsite," reliably ratioed into another dimension.
- Vrellnath: "we have reviewed the archival record of 2037 through 2049 and we are still worried about you. we did not say anything at the time. we should have said something at the time."
- Glœm: "we have read the records of the time when the posts went away. we think it was very sad. we have composed a song about it. we perform the song. we are sorry it was sad."
- A @dril post from 2034 screenshotted with the caption "he tried to warn us that it was about to go."
- Golden Age nostalgia, sincere and allowed: "my great-great-grandmother said the platform worked perfectly for forty years and moderation was timely and search found the post you were looking for and i do not believe her but i want to." Also: "whoever you were, thank you for the forty years. we still talk about you. we hope you are okay."
- On The Upload: "every single bad thing that has happened to the platform has happened within arm's reach of this man." From his Defenders: "people keep bringing up the '49 rocket like it's relevant. read a book. that was a FORMER EMPLOYEE'S component. he has DISTANCE from this." Under any statement of his involving a disaster: "Ex-Employee Defense just dropped."
- On any Upload announcement touching the platform: "every 200 years, on schedule. set your clocks," "clockwork," or "@dril called it in 3017 and nobody listened, again."
- A current bug posted with the caption "and the worst part is we're going to laugh about this in a decade," quote-posted by someone from two hundred years earlier laughing about the previous one.
- Complaints about the interface, conducted exactly as such complaints have always been conducted ("can't believe they changed the quantum entanglement button to a rhombus").
