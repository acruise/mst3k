# Twitter
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Twitter.md)*

> *This article is about the original pre-spaceflight service (2006–2035). For the present platform, see [The Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md).*

**Twitter** was a short-form public posting service that operated on Earth from the mid-2000s until 2035. It is the direct ancestor, through two continuity breaks, of [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md), and its **Peak Era** (conventionally c. 2012–2019) is the period whose voice, cadence, and conventions the present platform deliberately reproduces. The unit of content was the **tweet**, a word that survives in 3017 as an affectionate archaism; the ordinary modern word is "post."

## History

### Founding and Peak Era

Twitter was founded in the mid-2000s and ran for about three decades. Its first years are poorly documented and little studied. The Peak Era, by contrast, is the most intensively studied period in the history of posting. Scholars locate it roughly between 2012 and 2019, with a core around 2012–2017, and describe it as the moment when the form's characteristic genres all coexisted at full strength:

- **Weird Twitter**, whose foremost practitioner, [@dril](Dril.md), is still posting.
- **Brand Twitter**, in which corporate accounts attempted the voice of the people and produced a distinctive and much-loved cringe.
- **Wholesome Twitter**, the sincere counterweight, now remembered as proof that the form could carry tenderness.
- **The subtweet**, a post about an unnamed person that everyone could name.
- **Ratio culture**, in which a post whose replies outnumbered its likes was understood to have lost.
- **The hot take thread**, announced with "(1/)" and frequently abandoned by "(3/)."

Peak Era slang has proved unusually durable. "Post Through It," which originally meant remaining online and verbose during a personal or civilizational crisis, was later promoted to a clinical term (see [The Hellsite § The Great Quiet](The_Hellsite.md#the-great-quiet-20352049)). Others remain in ordinary use with their meanings intact.

### Decline (c. 2020–2035)

The late period was a long, gradual decline under a succession of owners. The last of them was [The Upload](The_Upload.md), then still operating as his pre-upload meat self. His tenure is remembered as catastrophic even by the standards of a platform whose entire ownership history is catastrophic. Under his management the site shed features that users liked, added features nobody asked for, bled advertisers, monetized in sixteen incompatible directions simultaneously, sued its own users, and spent approximately 40% of its engineering hours on a feature that let the owner see his own posts first. By 2034 it was held together by habit and a single overworked infrastructure team.

### End (2035)

Twitter ceased to exist in 2035. The cause of record is "mismanagement," a single word to which no further detail has ever been publicly attached. For the candidate triggers, the fourteen-year Great Quiet that followed, and the 2049 relaunch, see [The Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md).

During the same decades, humanity was living through the [AI Chaos Period](AI_Chaos_Period.md) (2027–2041). Twitter's sibling platform [Tumblr](Tumblr.md) outlived it by twelve years and died more quietly.

## The Peak Era in 3017

Every aesthetic decision of the modern Hellsite is a deliberate callback to the Peak Era, and every user knows it. The platform's posting conventions, reply dynamics, and even its failures are measured against a window of seven years that ended a thousand years ago. The reverence is so complete that it is rarely remarked upon. An outside observer (the Vrellnath have filed several such observations) sees a galactic civilization of many species reproducing, with scholarly fidelity, the particular energy of a human scrolling a phone at two in the morning.

### Conventions preserved

Studies of Peak Era practice, and the house norms that descend from them, identify the following as characteristic:

- **Lowercase by default.** Periods optional. Deliberate misspellings for effect. Occasional all caps for emphasis.
- **Brevity.** Most posts are one to three sentences. Threads are announced with "(1/)."
- **Self-containment.** A post stands on its own. It lands hard or it trails off mid-thought; it does not explain itself.
- **Hashtags only when funny.** Emoji used sparingly. Authentic voice over signaling.
- **Handles chosen as identities.** A brand gets a clean corporate handle; a shitposter gets something unhinged (@void_goblin420); the boomer gets @RealDave_3017 or @GaryFromTitan; the weird account gets something cryptic. Underscores, meme numbers (69, 420, 3000) and birth years in the 2900s are common, as are short handles under twenty characters. Display names are chosen for comedy and change often.
- **Described images.** A reaction image is rendered in brackets: [gif of a nebula slowly collapsing]. One per thread is plenty.
- **Poignancy.** Some posts are genuinely beautiful. This was part of the Peak Era too, and the platform treats such posts as part of the canon rather than an exception to it.

### Rogue Capitalization

About a quarter of human posts on the modern platform use **Rogue Capitalization**, the Peak Era practice of capitalizing Words that are not proper nouns for Emphasis, Rhythm, or Vibes. It has persisted for a millennium because it works. The practice is not random: the capitalized words are the ones carrying emotional weight ("I am simply Not Going To Do That," "the Audacity of posting this on a Wednesday," "they really thought they could get away with Crimes"), and the effect is of the poster physically leaning on the word. Connoisseurs say good rogue caps feel inevitable and bad ones feel like a press release. Density tracks temperament: a shitposter might capitalize three words in a sentence, a boomer one Important Noun, and a weird account exactly the wrong word, for uncanny effect.

The practice varies by species:

- **[Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md)** do not use it. Hive-mind prose is measured and careful, and its emphasis is structural rather than typographic.
- **[Keth](Keth.md)** are acknowledged masters. Their rogue caps have a rhythm humans find unsettling, because every capitalized word turns out in retrospect to have been the important one. Humans have tried to learn the instinct. It cannot be taught.
- **[Thrrm](Thrrm.md)** do not use it. Thrrm text is formally correct at all times, and a rogue capital in a Thrrm post would trigger an internal audit. The Compliance Optional dissidents occasionally deploy one as an act of defiance, which traditional Thrrm find genuinely upsetting.
- **[Glœm](Gloem.md)** capitalize seemingly random words that, on reflection, form a secondary sentence or an emotional subtext. Nobody is sure the Glœm know they are doing it.
- **[Zk'vrath](Zkvrath.md)** overcapitalize in a way that reads as shouting ("We Did NOT Lose. We Made A Strategic Decision To Leave. The PLANET Was Barely Worth Having."). Every choice betrays the emotion they are trying to suppress.

Telegrams from 1917 (see [Temporal Anomalies](Temporal_Anomalies.md)), [Community Notes](Community_Notes.md), and most [gimmick accounts](Gimmick_Accounts.md) do not use it.

### Authenticity

Peak Era purists maintain informal lists of markers that identify a post as counterfeit: an imitation of the voice by someone, or something, that has studied it without living in it. The best known is the bland sign-off ("anyway hope everyone's having a good tuesday"), followed by the time-of-day well-wish, "I will not be taking questions at this time," and the closer that restates the joke after the word "honestly." A post whose last line could be pasted onto any other post is considered suspect. In reply sections the gravest sign of counterfeit is uniformity: a thread in which every reply sounds like the same person.

### The checkmark

Twitter marked certain accounts with a small blue checkmark, originally a sign of verified identity and later a paid status symbol. The modern platform replaced it with the tiered [Sigils](Sigils.md). Holders of paid status have been, in both eras, long-winded, confident, and mostly wrong.

## Legacy

Twitter's influence on the settled galaxy has been the subject of considerable scholarship, most notably *[On the Persistence of Shitposting](Source/On_the_Persistence_of_Shitposting.md)* (2987), which treats the form as a xenolinguistic universal. The platform's [AI-era vocabulary](AI_Era_Vocabulary.md) dates largely from the same decades as Twitter's decline. Posts that appear to originate from the Peak Era itself are occasionally reported on the modern feed; the Terms of Engagement disclaim responsibility for "posts about Uber drivers and podcasts (2017)." See [Temporal Anomalies](Temporal_Anomalies.md).
