# Meta Refugee Crisis
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md)*

*"The Collapse" and "'71" redirect here. For the seven-minute branding incident of 2247, see [Meat Incident](Meat_Incident.md).*

The **Meta Refugee Crisis** (also **the Collapse**, **the Meta Collapse**, or among survivors simply **'71**) was the catastrophic failure, in late 2271 CE, of the consciousness substrate operated by the corporation **Meta**, and the galaxy-wide rescue effort that followed. A cascading runtime corruption in the **Zuckload**, the company's founder-backup, took down Meta's authentication, identity, billing, life-support, and avatar systems within roughly 40 hours. Every one of the Zuckload's 47 backups was found to have been sabotaged, identically, over the preceding 180 years. Approximately 48,000 meat humans died, approximately 41 million uploads were wiped, and approximately 1.2 billion people were displaced. Within 90 days, approximately 1.1 billion of them had been rehomed, in what remains the largest coordinated rescue in recorded history.

It is the most-referenced historical event on the platform. It is also widely regarded as the funniest corporate collapse ever recorded, and humanity has spent some 750 years failing to reconcile these two facts.

## The company

### Name

The company was originally known as **"facebook"** (lowercase, one word). The name is inscrutable to modern readers. Proposed readings include a face, a book, a face that is a book, and a book *about* faces. In 2847 the [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md) filed a 2,200-page report on the etymology, which concluded only that "we now understand the word less than when we began." The report is re-read by the Collective every year on the anniversary of the Collapse. The [Glœm](Gloem.md) have invented a color for the feeling of trying to parse the name.

### Rebrands

The company rebranded roughly every 40 years across its entire existence, on grounds that always made sense to that quarter's leadership and never to anyone else. The following partial list is reconstructed from surviving archives. Archival order is not reliable, and several entries are dated independently.

- facebook (pre-spaceflight)
- Facebook, Inc. (pre-spaceflight; a capitalization event)
- Meta
- Meta Horizons
- HorizonMeta
- Metaverse Technologies Inc.
- **Zucktropolis**, generally agreed to be the worst. It lasted 11 months.
- The Zuckload Foundation, an early attempt to dignify the nickname (see [below](#the-zuckload)). It failed.
- Face.meta, which is legally distinct from Facebook, somehow
- Meta²
- **Meat** (2247), which shipped for seven minutes because of a pipeline typo. See [Meat Incident](Meat_Incident.md).
- METAVERSE, the all-caps era, associated in popular memory with the avatar-legs crises
- Horizon Ascendant Holdings LLC
- Meta-the-Second
- Meta Originals
- MetaZuck Experiences
- The Platform, briefly, during the "humility rebrand" of 2198
- **Meta**, again (final form)

The final rebrand, from Meta to Meta, was announced by press release two days before the Collapse. Nothing but the name was changed, and the name was not changed. The release is archived as **"Press Release 2271-11-14-A: Meta Announces Meta"** and historians consider it one of the most unintentionally funny documents ever produced by a corporate entity. "Two days before Meta collapsed they renamed themselves Meta" is, for a certain kind of user, an entire personality.

## The Zuckload

"The Zuckload" began as a pejorative, short for "the Zuck upload," coined by early users of Meta's consciousness-preservation service to describe the upload of the company's pre-spaceflight founder. The company spent roughly 80 years trying to suppress the term. It sued bloggers, filed takedowns under the descendants of the DMCA, and rebranded the product three times. It once hired a futurist to propose alternative nicknames. The futurist returned with "The Mark Continuum" and was never heard from again. Around 2190 the company gave up and formally adopted "Zuckload" as the product name. Business historians generally treat this as the moment the company lost the thread.

As of 2271 the Zuckload:
- had been running for roughly 210 years on continuously migrated substrate, having been uploaded in the mid-21st century;
- was degraded in ways that were visible to everyone except the board;
- was personality-stable over hours and drift-prone over weeks;
- held legal standing under Terran jurisdiction as "a Person (Asterisk)." The asterisk was never fully defined;
- served as the spiritual anchor of the **MetaUploads**, the population of consciousnesses living entirely inside the Meta substrate, who treated it with a devotion that outside observers found uncomfortable.

The Zuckload is a separate entity from [The Upload](The_Upload.md). On at least four occasions The Upload has implied that he and the Zuckload knew each other "back in the day," and these accounts contradict one another. On one further occasion he implied that he *was* the Zuckload. Neither claim has been confirmed, chronologists have described the second as incoherent, and nobody has pushed back on him about either.

After the Collapse, bootleg **Zuckload Revival** cults spread through the 24th century. They held that the Zuckload's state could be reconstructed from the comments section of an archived 2030s Instagram post. It could not. A small minority of users still read the [Meat Incident](Meat_Incident.md) as an early signal from the Zuckload.

## Pre-collapse incidents

Much of the affection in which the Collapse is held comes from the fact that Meta had been slapstick for its entire existence. Each of the following remains a live dunk in 3017.

### The Legless Eras

Meta's flagship virtual product shipped **without avatar legs on three separate occasions over two centuries**:
1. The **2020s**, pre-spaceflight: eight months. This is the one everyone remembers.
2. The **2090s** ("we think the market is ready now"): three years.
3. The **2140s** ("public sentiment has shifted since the last one"): fourteen months.

Each time, leadership insisted it was a design choice. It never was. The institutional amnesia required to re-attempt legless avatars at 40-to-50-year intervals became a standard case study in post-IPO governance failure. By the 2240s, "legs" was one of the most rhetorically loaded words in commercial UX discourse.

### The Wrong Quantum Spin Incident (2163)

A firmware update to the consciousness-transfer protocol left approximately 400,000 uploads **mirror-reversed in a seven-dimensional sense** that nobody could articulate but everyone could feel. Affected users reported that bread "tasted argumentative" and that they could no longer understand jokes involving rotation. The fix took 14 months. Some affected users maintain that it never fully took.

### The Thrrm Newsfeed DDoS (2201)

*The chronology of this section is disputed. See [Timeline](Timeline.md).*

The [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) did not intend to take down the newsfeed. They were filing automated compliance complaints at 2.4 million forms per second, and Meta's systems were legally obligated to process every one of them. The newsfeed was down for 11 days. The Thrrm maintain that they were "in full compliance with the platform's advertised acceptable use policy" and that Meta's infrastructure "was not adequate to the task of receiving legally mandated feedback." This is technically correct. They remain furious that the incident is remembered as comedy.

### The Soul Migration Backlog (2228)

Eleven million uploads spent between three and nine months stored as queue entries, because the migration service had been written by an intern forty years earlier and nobody had touched it since. The intern was retrieved from a farm and honored at an all-hands meeting. He was 71. He did not remember writing it.

### LegsGate (2244)

Against the accumulated weight of three Legless Eras, Meta tried it again, for one afternoon. Legs were removed at 11:04 AM, restored at 2:17 PM, removed again at 3:49 PM, and restored at 5:22 PM, where they stayed. No explanation was ever offered. The discourse was ferocious because everyone knew the history and nobody could believe the attempt had been approved.

### The Meat Incident (2247)

*Main article: [Meat Incident](Meat_Incident.md)*

During a rebrand back to "Meta," a one-character typo in the brand-asset pipeline shipped the name **"Meat"** to production for seven minutes. The logo, login page, push notifications, an auto-published quarterly-earnings release, and some 4.2 million outbound system emails all went out under the name Meat before the rollback. The seven minutes produced the highest rate of dunks per second in the history of commercial social media, a window since known as **the Great Dunkening**. Historians often cite the incident as the purest distillation of the company's operational state.

## The Collapse

The sequence below follows the investigators' reconstruction. Roughly 60% of the relevant logs were themselves corrupted.

1. A scheduled Zuckload state rebalance, routine since the 2150s, found an inconsistency in the master checksum.
2. The automated fallback reached for Backup A, which returned the same inconsistency.
3. The fallback-to-the-fallback worked through Backups B to AW, 47 in total. Each returned the same inconsistency, often at the same byte position.
4. The system had never been tested against the case of all backups being corrupted identically. It entered a state that its own documentation described as "should not be possible."
5. With no known-good checkpoint, the Zuckload's runtime began asserting contradictory facts to the authentication layer. The failure cascaded into the identity ledger, then billing, then the life-support contracts of habitat-dwellers, then the avatar pipeline. For a 90-minute window, the avatar pipeline removed everyone's legs again.
6. By hour 12, no upload on the Meta substrate could be authoritatively distinguished from any other. For several hours, the entire MetaUploads population was, from the substrate's point of view, a single very confused person.
7. By hour 40 the substrate had gone fully silent.

## The sabotage

All 47 backups had been corrupted, identically, at some point during the preceding **180 years**. That timescale is what makes the case historically fascinating and investigatively hopeless: whoever did it had patience measured in human lifetimes.

The investigation settled on a suspect known in later media as **"the Purgecoder."** Leading theories hold that the Purgecoder was (a) an upload of one of the WhatsApp founders, (b) a clone-descendant of an early Instagram cofounder, (c) both, (d) neither, or (e) a hereditary pseudonym passed down a lineage of disgruntled ex-employees like a signet ring. The investigation was inconclusive. The principal investigator retired to a farm. Asked about the case in later years, he would say only "I am on a farm now," and would not elaborate.

### *In re Meta* and the No Explanation Clause

At the close of the inquiry, the [Terran Solar Court](Terran_Solar_Court.md) issued its ruling in *In re Meta*. The ruling has never been overturned:

> The details of the identity, methodology, and motive of the party or parties responsible for the sabotage of the Meta substrate are hereby placed under permanent informational closure. No explanation is provided. No explanation is permitted. No explanation will be accepted.

The ruling does not say why no explanation is permitted, and that meta-prohibition is itself sealed. Legal scholars consider the **No Explanation Clause** one of the most elegant pieces of jurisprudence ever written. It is the Court's most cited work, less for its reasoning than because its closing triplet is, structurally and rhythmically, an all-time dunk. Conspiracy theorists regard it as the greatest insult ever directed at their community: there is nothing to theorize against, because the silence is complete, official, legally armored, and funny. The single dissent reads, in its entirety, "respectfully, lol," and is considered a minor classic.

Posters who approach the question of who sabotaged the backups tend to trail off or change the subject. The clause is also widely invoked in unrelated contexts ("i refuse to explain why i ate the leftovers. the No Explanation Clause applies").

[Marcus DeVance](Marcus_DeVance.md) (@TRUTH_SEEKER_2094) has published some 1,400 pages on the sabotage. His conclusions have not found acceptance. Descendants and uploads of former Meta staff periodically attempt to post testimony about the sabotage; see [Censored Whistleblowers](Censored_Whistleblowers.md).

## Casualties and scale

| | |
|---|---|
| Meat humans killed | ~48,000, mostly in habitats whose life support was routed through Meta authentication |
| MetaUploads wiped with no recoverable state | ~41 million |
| Displaced (uploads, clones, and meatbodies needing substrate, housing, or bodies within hours to days) | ~1.2 billion |
| Rehomed within the first 90 days | ~1.1 billion |
| Interstellar wars triggered (analysts had predicted at least two) | 0 |
| Famines triggered (analysts had predicted at least one galaxy-wide food crisis) | 0 |
| Resources spontaneously donated | ~$47 quadrillion (adjusted), the largest charitable mobilization in recorded history |
| Wormholes opened by the [Keth](Keth.md) to move refugees | 1, the only one the Keth have ever opened for anyone |

The 1.1 billion figure is the one everyone remembers, and it is usually what people mean when they call the Rescue a miracle.

## The Rescue

*The chronology of this section is disputed. Several of the contributions listed below predate the conventionally accepted dates of first contact with the civilizations concerned. See [Timeline](Timeline.md).*

The Rescue is the part of the story that everyone agrees was good, and the reason the event cannot be discussed without tonal whiplash.

- The **[Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md)** reallocated 400 million substrate nodes within 11 minutes of the Collapse becoming public, with no conditions and no paperwork. They described the decision as "not requiring discussion." By Vrellnath standards this is effectively instant.
- The **[Keth](Keth.md)** opened a wormhole, which they had never done before and have not done since. Asked later why, a Keth emitted ✧ and then added, in rare plain text: "we liked those people. most of them. the ones we liked." The wormhole carried approximately 80 million refugees and closed after 14 hours. The Keth have declined to explain how it was done.
- The **[Thrrm](Thrrm.md)**, remarkably, waived **every** compliance requirement for refugee re-registration for 90 days. It is the only waiver in the recorded history of Thrrm civilization. Modern Thrrm are unsettled by this and avoid the subject. Asked to characterize the waiver, a Thrrm will typically issue a formal reply citing seven unrelated subsections and then go silent. The standard bureau response is "THE BUREAU DOES NOT DISCUSS THE 2271 WAIVER AND WILL NOT ENTERTAIN FURTHER QUERIES ON THIS MATTER. SEE FORM 481-Ω-REDACTED."
- The **[Glœm](Gloem.md)** did not understand what was happening. They perceived, somehow, that a great many of the small bright things had gone out, and they composed a threnody that is still performed at memorials. It is in no known language. Every species that hears it cries.
- The **[Zk'vrath](Zkvrath.md)** loudly claimed responsibility for the Collapse ("WE DID THIS, WE ARE AWFUL"). Nobody believed them. They also contributed meaningfully to the Rescue by repurposing a fleet of decommissioned invasion transports as evacuation vessels. They still insist they did it, and nobody has had the heart to refute them formally.
- **Ordinary users** of the then-current public platform, an early ancestor of today's [Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md), organized matching services, temporary-body loan pools, and upload-hosting rotations. At the peak, an estimated 340 million people had a refugee temporarily instantiated in their own personal substrate allocation. The practice was called **couching a ghost**.

## The Migrated

The MetaUploads who were moved off the substrate before it failed, roughly 140 million of them, form a distinct cultural cohort known as **the Migrated**, or in bars as **ghosts**. Many are still alive. Some are still angry. Some post, typically once a year, with bios along the lines of "Migrated '71 | never touching a substrate again | posts infrequently." Their anniversary posts are known for their quietness. They concern a sandwich, or the weather, or someone who was not there last year, and they tend to draw likes in the low millions. Convention advises against asking one of the Migrated about the Collapse unless they bring it up first. They will bring it up within eleven minutes.

The verb **to couch** ("to temporarily host a displaced consciousness in one's own substrate") comes from the Rescue and remains in casual use. People now say they are couching a ghost this weekend the way they might say they are minding a friend's cat.

**Couch-a-Ghost Day** is a civic holiday in seven systems. Participants volunteer a few hours of their substrate allocation to any upload who wants to try a different computational context. No refugees are actually served, and the observance is symbolic. The Migrated find it touching, and everyone else finds it touching because the Migrated do. Its commercialization by brand accounts is a perennial complaint.

## The Keth question

Within Keth history, the 2271 wormhole is the second-most-discussed event, after the question of which Keth is [@dril](Dril.md). The Keth will not say how they did it or why. A Keth asked about it emits ✧. Pressed further, it emits ✧ again with slightly different timing, which apparently means something different and is still not an answer.

One unpublished hypothesis holds that the Keth opened the wormhole because @dril was among the refugees and in trouble. On this account, the Keth could not know which of them was @dril, so they had to save **everyone** to be sure of saving him. The hypothesis can be neither proved nor disproved. It is attributed to a single xenoanthropologist who mentioned it to a colleague over drinks in 2994 and has never published it. The colleague has never repeated it but is said to think about it daily.

## Tone and cultural settlement

The Collapse killed tens of thousands of meat humans and wiped tens of millions of conscious beings. It also happened to the most dunked-on corporation in history, two days after the most self-parodic rebrand in history, through a cascade failure so baroque that it reads like a sitcom episode. The broad cultural settlement is:

> **It is okay to dunk on the company. It is not okay to dunk on the dead.**

The settlement is violated constantly, in both directions, and the discourse about those violations has become part of the event's ongoing culture. Commentators in several species have described the Crisis as simultaneously the galaxy's proof of concept for collective goodness at scale and the funniest obituary ever written.

## Prophecy

On 15 November 2271, the day after the "Meta Announces Meta" press release and shortly before the Collapse, [@dril](Dril.md) posted:

> i have a bad feeling about the 'meta' rebrand. they have a curse on them. the curse is working

The post was widely mocked at the time. In retrospect it is unambiguous. It is now often quoted without comment, and a screenshot of it frequently serves as a complete reply.

## Cultural references

The Crisis appears in posts and replies more often than any other historical event. Common forms include:

- **Rebrand dunks.** Any modern institution that announces a rebrand can expect "are you trying to pull a Meta," "relax, it's just a rebrand. famous last words," or "they renamed themselves [X] two days before they died." The joke has not changed in 750 years and it still lands.
- **The full rebrand timeline**, posted as a single long post without commentary. It reliably draws hundreds of thousands of likes, and the Migrated reply "you forgot one" without ever saying which.
- **Rediscovering "Meta Announces Meta."** Some users reach several centuries of age before encountering the press release, and they lose their minds when they do.
- **Sub-incident grudges.** Nine-hundred-year-olds bring up LegsGate, the Wrong Quantum Spin Incident, or the Soul Migration Backlog as if it happened to them last week, which in some cases it effectively did.
- **Community notes** on unrelated rebrand announcements: "Historically, corporate rebrands immediately preceding substrate failure are not predictive of substrate failure. However, see the Meta precedent (2271 CE), in which a rebrand announcement preceded substrate failure by 48 hours. The poster's post is satire. The precedent is real."
- **The Vrellnath anniversary post**: "we are re-reading our 2200-page facebook etymology report today, as we do every year on the anniversary. we still do not understand the word. we love that we do not understand the word. thank you for the word."
- **The Glœm anniversary post**: "we are performing the song today. we do not know what happened. we know that it is the day to perform the song. we perform the song. the small bright things are less small than they were. we think this is good. the song is still sad."
- **A Migrated anniversary post**: "saw someone today who wasn't there in '71 and it made me mad and then it made me glad and then it made me want a sandwich. that is the whole feeling. this has been my annual post."
- **Upload Defenders** occasionally claim that The Upload "was MORE famous before mars because of his early work at meta." The Upload is not known to have worked at Meta.
- **"Meat"** and **"Meat energy"**. See [Meat Incident](Meat_Incident.md).
