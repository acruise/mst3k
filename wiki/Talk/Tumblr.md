## Canon

- **Keep the assassination pointed but not crass.** "Forty bans for the crime of having sexuality while transfemme" is the core and stays intact. It is a joke, and it is also a real thing actual platforms did, remembered with the specific weight of something that wasn't taken seriously enough at the time.
- **Photomatt is named; the ex-user is not.** The asymmetry is canon: her name is remembered by the specific people who remember it; the broader culture remembers only the number.
- **Photomatt is the name of record. Never give him a civilian name.** The shorthand is all that survives. (Out of universe it's a real person's handle; the "no civilian name" rule also keeps us from naming a real individual as an assassination victim.)
- **The AGI reveal is load-bearing but offscreen.** Don't dramatize the discovery. In most posts it's a three-word reference ("you know, the Tumblr thing"), not a setpiece.
- **Do not conflate** the Tumblr AGI with the Hellsite-era "AI nobody realized was an AI for 60 years." Two separate events; the Tumblr one was earlier and more tragic.
- **Dates are canon:** servers dark late 2047 (a Thursday); estate auction 2048; AGI found 2197; Memory Protection Act 2197; repealed 2200.
- **The collector's identity** is "not supported by available evidence." Don't resolve.
- **Nobody says "Tumblr" directly in polite contexts** in 3017.
- **Distinct from Bluesky.** Both are platforms that exceeded their spec, but Tumblr's AGI was retroactive and destroyed (tragic); Bluesky's Ascension is ongoing and untouchable (transcendent). Different events, different moods.

## Voice and usage

Callback shapes (from the bible):
- "Forty Bans energy" on any disproportionate moderation decision. Nobody under 400 understands it; everyone under 400 uses it.
- A moderation-policy thread derailed by "you know what happened to photomatt": somewhere between threat, warning, and historical observation, and nobody's ever sure which.
- An AI ethics thread citing "the Tumblr Case" in passing; one reply "the repeal was the atrocity, not the Act"; another "the Act WAS the atrocity"; a fourth just "yes."
- A post about the soft-reblog or wrong-color-text-under-image format, credited to "a dead platform whose name is not used in polite company for reasons."

Keep the tone rule from the Canon section in mind: the joke is on platforms and panicked legislatures, never on the banned user.

## Discrepancies

- **Timing of the assassination.** The bible says "mid-2040s"; the platform outlived Photomatt by "about seven months" and went dark "late 2047," which puts the assassination in early 2047. "Mid-2040s" is loose but compatible. The article keeps "mid-2040s."
- **"While Twitter was dying loudly under The Upload's ownership."** Both the bible and the system prompt frame Tumblr's death as simultaneous with Twitter's; Twitter ended in 2035 and Tumblr in 2047, twelve years apart, during the Great Quiet. The article says only "while Twitter was dying loudly" in the lead.
- **Collector's holding "accessible for a century and a half" vs. destroyed "early."** The databases were publicly accessible from 2048 to about 2197 (fine), and the collector's copy was destroyed early under the Act. Consistent; noted because the bible says both "the collector did preserve the databases" and "the private collector's holding was destroyed early."
