# Terran Solar Court
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Terran_Solar_Court.md)*

The **Terran Solar Court** is the galaxy's highest court for any matter involving humans. For centuries its bench has been continuously stacked with justices who are unquestioned scholars of **shitposting and dunking**. The Court is best known for the **No Explanation Clause** of *In re Meta*, for rulings that are terse, rhythmically devastating, and frequently quoted, and for one-line dissents that enter the canon.

The [Thrrm](Thrrm.md) are legally required to acknowledge the Court's rulings, and they despise it more than any other institution in the galaxy.

## The bench

The most important qualification for a seat on the Court is not jurisprudential expertise. It is **demonstrated mastery** of four things:

- **the load-bearing pause**
- **the refusal-as-statement**
- **the reply that ends a debate**
- **the ratio as a form of equitable remedy**

Appointment to the Terran Solar Court is widely regarded as the galaxy's most prestigious literary award. Legal commentators and literary critics review the Court's output with roughly equal seriousness and frequently reach the same conclusions.

The theory behind this, to the extent anyone has articulated one, is that for most of human history the people best at ending an argument in a single sentence have been posters rather than lawyers. The Court has not been asked to justify its composition. It is not clear that it would.

## Style of rulings

The Court's rulings are short. They are built for rhythm. Many are meme-able, and a meaningful number were apparently written to be. The Court's jurisprudence is quoted on the platform more often than any other body of law, and quoted accurately, which legal scholars regard as the real achievement.

Dissents are frequently a single line. The most famous consists of two words and a comma.

## *In re Meta* and the No Explanation Clause

The Court's most cited ruling arose from the inquiry into the sabotage of the Meta substrate, whose collapse in 2271 wiped some 41 million uploaded minds and killed around 48,000 embodied humans (see [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md)). Every one of the substrate's 47 backups had been corrupted identically at some point in the preceding 180 years. The investigation settled on a suspect known as "the Purgecoder," whose identity was never established.

At the conclusion of the inquiry, the Court issued the following ruling, which has never been overturned:

> The details of the identity, methodology, and motive of the party or parties responsible for the sabotage of the Meta substrate are hereby placed under permanent informational closure. No explanation is provided. No explanation is permitted. No explanation will be accepted.

This is the **No Explanation Clause.** The ruling does not explain why no explanation is permitted. The meta-prohibition is itself sealed.

Legal scholars consider it one of the most elegant pieces of jurisprudence ever written. It is the Court's most cited work, though not because of its legal reasoning: *"No explanation is provided. No explanation is permitted. No explanation will be accepted."* is, structurally and rhythmically, an all-time dunk.

Conspiracy theorists consider it the greatest insult to conspiracy theorists ever devised, because there is nothing to theorize against. The silence is complete, official, legally armored, and funny. At least one theorist, Marcus DeVance ([@TRUTH_SEEKER_2094](Marcus_DeVance.md)), has nonetheless produced 1,400 pages on the subject.

### The dissent

One justice dissented. The dissent reads, in its entirety:

> respectfully, lol

It is considered a minor classic.

### Legacy

The Clause is invoked on the platform as a punchline in contexts that have nothing to do with Meta ("i refuse to explain why i ate the leftovers. the No Explanation Clause applies"). Invoking it is understood to end the conversation.

The ruling's name is also attached to later litigation over the Meta collapse, which remains the subject of extensive legal scholarship. See [Meta Refugee Crisis](Meta_Refugee_Crisis.md).

## Other matters

The Court's docket is largely sealed, and what is known about it is known mostly from what it declined to say:

- **Sealed rulings on startups.** The Court has issued sealed rulings on several ventures of the [Startup Bro Constellation](Startup_Bro_Constellation.md), generally those in the dystopian category.
- **The Mars filing.** The Court has declined to take a filing concerning the chronological proximity between the death of the commune that relaunched the Hellsite, which died on its way to Mars, and [The Upload](The_Upload.md)'s later administration of Mars. See [The Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md). Nobody presses the question.
- **Sealed orders and whistleblowers.** Terran Solar Court sealed orders are one of four commonly proposed sources of the redactions on posts by [censored whistleblowers](Censored_Whistleblowers.md), alongside The Upload's legal team, the Meta estate trust, and the platform's own compliance stack. Nobody has received a straight answer about which is responsible. Asking makes the redactions worse.

## Relationship with the Thrrm

The Thrrm are legally required to acknowledge Court rulings. They regard the Court as a body that issues binding judgments without citing a single form, in a register indistinguishable from the one they spend their professional lives correcting. Of all the institutions they must defer to, they despise this one most. Thrrm procedural filings that reference Court rulings are noted for their extreme formal correctness and their total absence of warmth.

## In popular culture

The Court is followed on the platform the way sports are followed elsewhere.

- Posters **quote rulings like song lyrics.**
- Pending decisions are **speculated about the way fans speculate about drafts.**
- Comment sections are periodically devastated by **anonymous accounts believed to be clerks.** The clerks have never been confirmed. The devastation has.
- When a ruling drops, **"the Court is cooking"** trends.
- "respectfully, lol" is used as a standalone reply to end an argument while conceding it.
