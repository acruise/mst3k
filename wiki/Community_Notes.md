# Community Notes
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Community_Notes.md)*

**Community notes** are the platform's contributor-driven context system. When a post contains a specific false or misleading claim, other users may attach a **note**: a calm, sourced, professionally worded correction that appears in a grey box beneath the post. A note is rated by contributors along a large and growing set of **rating dimensions**, of which only two or three are shown on any given view, selected by an algorithm whose logic nobody has been able to explain.

The system is one of the oldest continuously operating features on the platform. It has been extended by every species that has joined it and simplified by none.

## Anatomy of a note

A standard note has four parts:

1. **A header.** Community-written notes open with the traditional formula *"Readers added context they thought people might want to know."* Notes from other contributor classes carry their own headers (see [Contributor types](#contributor-types)).
2. **The correction.** Usually one or two paragraphs. The house style is calm, fair, and slightly apologetic: "This post contains multiple factual errors," "The claim that X is misleading because…" Notes are never snarky and never combative.
3. **Citations**, numbered [1], [2], [3]. Sources range from the authoritative (*Galactic Transit Authority Compliance Bulletin 4,891-B*) to the personal (*Dr. Chen-Okafor, personal correspondence*) to, on occasion, *vibes*.
4. **Surfaced ratings and a rater count**, e.g. *"14,203 contributors rated this note · ratings surfaced by algorithm."* Rater counts typically run from a few hundred to tens of thousands.

A representative example, attached to a post concerning bread:

> ⚠ **Readers added context they thought people might want to know**
> … [4] Commercial bread products do not contain ferret meat (Galactic Food Safety Bureau, ongoing inspections).
>
> ✓ Epistemically Sound 94% · ⊙ Ontologically Grounded 88% · ◈ Pre-Verified by Consensus —

## History

The system descends from a context feature of the original [Twitter](Twitter.md), whose notes were rated on a single binary: **Helpful** or **Not Helpful**. Archival examples of the binary system survive. Modern readers tend to find them unsettling, in the way one might find a legal document from a civilization that had not yet invented nuance.

The oldest rating dimensions still in use date to **2094**, the beginning of what is now called the Classical period. From there the system accreted. Each era of human expansion, and each contact event, added dimensions; almost none were ever removed. Several were added by committees whose records are lost. At least one was added as a joke and made permanent. By 3017 there are roughly **thirty** official dimensions, plus an unknown number of unofficial ones that appear anyway.

## The surfacing algorithm

Only **two or three** dimensions are shown on any view of a note. Which ones appear is decided by an algorithm that nobody has been able to explain, including the platform. The same note may surface different ratings on different views. A note about a photograph of a dog may surface *Does Not Provoke the Zk'vrath: 98%*. A note correcting a conspiracy theory may pair *Epistemically Sound 94%* with *Does Not Alarm the Glœm 11%*. A Thrrm compliance note may show *Thrrm Compliant 100%* beside *Feels Right 3%*.

Complaints about the algorithm have been filed continuously for several centuries. None has succeeded. This is the platform.

*Pre-Verified by Consensus* is surfaced regularly, and always reads "—". Users have learned to ignore it.

## What gets noted

Notes are uncommon. The system is understood to recognize humor, mostly, and notes are attached only where a specific claim is false or misleading. Typical subjects:

- conspiracy theories shared in good faith ("wormholes are just holograms");
- confidently wrong posts from holders of [Tier 2 and Tier 3 sigils](Sigils.md);
- specific, verifiable hallucinations by platform AI assistants;
- Thrrm citing the wrong subsection of the wrong form;
- federation requests from [external instances](External_Federation_Attempts.md), which receive notes explaining, with great tenderness, that federation is still not possible;
- any post attempting to list the phrases of the ChantGPT Anti-Summoning (see [AI Chaos Period](AI_Chaos_Period.md)), which is noted immediately.

Occasionally a note is attached to a post that later turns out to have been true. The note is not removed.

### The negation convention

Notes are written to negate specific claims rather than to assert alternatives. The canonical example is the formula used on posts by Marcus DeVance ([@TRUTH_SEEKER_2094](Marcus_DeVance.md)) concerning the identity of the @dril account: *"No extraordinary claim about the @dril account's identity is supported by available evidence."* The notes do not state what the account is; they state only that DeVance's claims about it are unsupported. Note-writers have understood the distinction between "no evidence supports X" and "X is false" for four centuries and regard it as good epistemic hygiene.

DeVance's posts are among the most heavily noted on the platform. Early notes on them were curt ("This account has a long history of misinformation. See: [link]"). By the four-hundred-year mark they had become multi-paragraph essays with fifteen citations, and they routinely score above 90% on *Epistemically Sound*. When DeVance reposted a paper that used him as its principal example of a discredited source, believing it vindicated him, the note beneath his repost explained, patiently, that he had misread the paper. One of his posts, concerning an encounter in a produce section, has never been noted. The replies to it are mostly "hope you're okay man."

### Anomalous content

The note system has no framework for pre-digital content. Notes attached to [1917 transmissions](Temporal_Anomalies.md) attempt explanation from historical archives in a tone of barely concealed bewilderment, typically beginning *"This transmission predates all known community context systems."*

Posts from the [Hells leak](Hells_Leak.md) have proved worse. A typical hell-post accumulates a stack of five conflicting notes: one filed ("this is a creative writing post"), one retracting it ("this appears to be a real legacy contract artifact"), one refiling it, a Thrrm attempt to close discussion under a form that does not cover the case, and a replacement form rejected by an auditor on grounds eight hundred years out of date. None of them is the note the post needs.

### @dril

The @dril account has never received a community note.

## Deranged topics

For reasons that are historically traceable but have never been corrected, the note system is disproportionately intense about exactly two subjects. Most posts touching these subjects receive ordinary notes or none. When the intensity does engage, the note abandons its own conventions in recognizable ways.

### Moons Notes

A post mentioning any moon, in any context, may receive a **Moons Note**. Hallmarks:

- six or more paragraphs rather than one or two, with twenty to forty citations, several of which cannot be located;
- five to eight surfaced rating dimensions instead of two or three, as though the algorithm has given up and is showing everything;
- a tone that begins normally and drifts into pleading: *"we respectfully beg the poster to reconsider their characterization of lunar bodies"*;
- oblique references to the 2040s prediction of a second moon and its confirmation in 2891, **without naming the predictor**; the committee will not say the name in this context and will not explain why;
- the dimension *Does Not Reference the Thing Inside Jupiter*, surfaced at a low percentage whether or not it is relevant;
- a pathological version history: *"[this note has been updated 47 times]"*, *"[previous versions available upon request (they are not)]"*.

The moon committee is understood to carry unresolved historical trauma. Nobody has addressed this, because nobody wants to be the person who touches the moon committee.

### Dog Notes

A post mentioning any dog, in any context, may receive a **Dog Note**. Dog Notes are tender rather than corrective. Hallmarks:

- *Dogs Would Like This* surfaced at 98–100%, whether or not the post concerns a dog;
- dimensions that do not officially exist: *Good Boy Quotient: HIGH*, *The Dog Is Well: Confirmed*, *Tail Status: Wagging*;
- citations to the Galactic Canine Welfare Consortium, Dr. Paws-Whitfield, and the 2847 Dog Accord, none of which can be located;
- content concerned mainly with the dog rather than the post's claim;
- a closing reassurance regardless of subject: *"the dog is well," "the dog is fine," "the dog has been notified and is pleased."*

The committee has declined to fix Dog Notes on three separate occasions, because ratings for "fixed" Dog Notes are always lower than for unfixed ones.
