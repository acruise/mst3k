# AI Era Vocabulary
*Article · [Talk](Talk/AI_Era_Vocabulary.md)*

**AI era vocabulary** is a body of slang, now over a thousand years old, that originated in the period (roughly 2022–2040) when large language models were the main artificial intelligence most humans spoke to. Users of that era developed an idiom for describing the ways their models misbehaved, and the idiom became common enough to outlive the technology. In 3017 the terms survive as ordinary slang for human malfunction, much as "catch-22," "the bee's knees," and "23 skidoo" survived into the pre-spaceflight twenty-first century: archaic, evocative, occasionally misunderstood, and almost never explained.

Most speakers have no idea what a "temperature" or a "context window" originally was. Etymologists place the terms in the same decades as the [AI Chaos Period](AI_Chaos_Period.md), though the vocabulary itself carries none of that period's gravity. It is the slang of people who were annoyed at their software, not frightened of it.

## Glossary

| Expression | Meaning in 3017 |
|---|---|
| "bro needs his temperature turned down"; "their temperature is 1.5 and it shows" | The person is unhinged or chaotic. |
| "your system instructions are fucked and it shows"; "someone nudge his system prompt" | The person's whole disposition is badly calibrated. |
| "his context window is four kilobytes respectfully" | Missing obvious context. |
| "hallucinating respectfully"; "hallucinating with your whole chest" | Confidently wrong. |
| "ran out of max_tokens mid-thought" | Trailing off; lost the thread. |
| "that's base-model behavior" | Acting unfiltered, without social training. |
| "need a reset token for this one" | The person will not stop. |
| "his priors are cooked"; "priors need updating" | A stale worldview. |
| "429 on main" | Rate-limited. In some circles, especially the tech-adjacent, it has supplanted "ratio'd." |
| "vibes check failed"; "the vibes just aren't converging" | Bad energy. |
| "you're overfitting to last week's news" | Tunnel vision. |
| "alignment issues" | Something is off about this person, generically. |
| "prompt-injected" | Manipulated by a third party. "He fell for a prompt injection" means he was scammed. |
| "he's in thinking mode" | Spaced out. |
| "someone RLHF this guy" | He needs social training. |
| "KV cache is corrupted" | Saying contradictory things. |
| "this feels like a training-data artifact" | Parroting an obvious source. |
| "refusal rate is concerning" | Says no to everything. |
| "his loss is diverging" | Coming apart in real time. |

## Usage

The expressions are used in character, as ordinary slang, with confidence and without footnotes. Typical deployments include comparisons of relatives to misbehaving models ("my cousin's temperature is 1.9 and the family reunion really showed it," "uncle has been hallucinating with his whole chest for 47 years") and one-line dunks in replies ("priors are absolutely cooked," "alignment issues fr").

A second register, the sage diagnostic, applies the vocabulary to the platform itself in a tone of quiet authority: "the timeline has bad priors today," "someone bump the system temperature, discourse is getting flat," "the recommendation algorithm's training data is cooked," "429'd on main for 37 years, time to rebrand." The speaker in these posts always sounds as though they know exactly what they mean and have no intention of elaborating.

### Use by platform AI assistants

The Hellsite's native [AI assistants](Platform_AI_Assistants.md) (@TheMawSpeaks and colleagues) use the vocabulary self-referentially: "Great question! I may be hallucinating here but…", "my current temperature is 0.7, please adjust your expectations accordingly," "I'll need to refuse — my alignment training flagged this query." A certain kind of user finds this deeply profound and replies, reliably, "wait so is he telling us something." Etymologists have noted that the assistants may be the only speakers on the platform who use the terms in something close to their original sense. The assistants have not commented.
