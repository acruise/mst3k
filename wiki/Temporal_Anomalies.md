# Temporal Anomalies
*Article · [Talk](Talk/Temporal_Anomalies.md)*

*This article is about posts arriving from other eras. For posts arriving from a hosted afterlife, see [Hells Leak](Hells_Leak.md). For skeets leaking from the ascended remnant of Bluesky, see [Bluesky](Bluesky.md).*

**Temporal anomalies** are posts that appear in the feed of [the Hellsite](The_Hellsite.md) but originate in another era. They are colloquially said to have "fallen through a wormhole," although no wormhole has ever been located and the phrase is used in the same spirit as "the algorithm." Three source eras are recognized: **1917**, **2017**, and **2517**. Each arrives with its own visual character, its own relationship to the platform's metrics, and its own peculiar effect on the 3017 audience.

Temporal anomalies make up a small fraction of the feed. The most frequently quoted estimate, "about one post in twenty," is almost certainly too high and is repeated mainly because nobody has produced a better one.[citation needed] Anomalies arrive both as top-level posts and, more unsettlingly, as replies inside ordinary threads.

## Mechanism

The mechanism is unknown. There is no accepted account of how text from 1917, which was written on paper and transmitted by wire, if it was transmitted at all, comes to be rendered in a feed that did not exist until 2049. The 2017 and 2517 anomalies are less mysterious in medium, since both eras had networked posting, but no more explicable in transit.

Several observations are routinely cited in the literature:

- **The Keth have been suspiciously quiet.** The [Keth](Keth.md), who are old enough to have opinions about most things, have posted nothing about temporal anomalies that scholars can identify as being about them. Given how the Keth usually behave, this silence is itself treated as data.
- **[@dril](Dril.md) has never acknowledged them.** Nobody has found a post, like, or reply by @dril that engages with any anomaly from any era.
- **The platform's own infrastructure does not know what to do.** Anomalous posts are rendered through fallback paths that surface diagnostic text to the reader. Typical examples, collected by enthusiasts:
  - `ERR:TEMPORAL_ROUTING origin_epoch outside indexable range (1917) · metrics_engine returned NULL · routing to fallback renderer`
  - `CONTENT_EPOCH_MISMATCH … no metrics schema available · display_mode:DEGRADED`
  - `WARN:FEED_RENDERER unrecognized content epoch · avatar_service: NO_MATCH · handle_registry: PRE_DIGITAL · rendering in compatibility mode`
  - `TEMPORAL_INTEGRITY_FAULT at feed/render.q:4471 — content origin (1917 CE) precedes platform instantiation · no metrics · no sigil · no handle · [filed under Form 91-Kappa, pending 40yr review]`
  - `SIGTERM in metrics_worker — epoch 1917 not in valid range — … falling back to raw_content_display — this is probably fine`
- **The seam.** When the feed places posts from two different eras next to each other, a faint visible distortion appears between them. Users call it "the seam." It has no documented function.

## 1917

The 1917 anomalies are the oldest, the strangest, and by general agreement the most affecting. They are genuine First World War–era text, from a ground-based armed conflict on Earth,[^gmha] in one of two forms.

### Telegrams

Telegrams arrive on manila paper in a typewriter face, in capital letters, with **STOP** between clauses and no articles. The sender line takes the place of a display name: a rank, a name, and a unit or location, such as `CPL. E. WHITFIELD, 3RD BTN., SOMME SECTOR` or `MRS. A. PEMBERTON, LONDON SW1`. There is no handle, no avatar, no [sigil](Sigils.md), and no metrics. The metrics engine returns nothing, and the platform has stopped trying. Many telegrams carry a period stamp such as PASSED BY CENSOR, FIELD POST, PRIORITY, RECEIVED, CLEARED or OFFICIAL.

The canonical example, reproduced on more merchandise than any other anomaly, is:

> SITUATION IN YPRES DETERIORATING STOP MORALE LOW STOP SEND SOCKS STOP

### Letters

Personal letters arrive on paler paper in a cramped hand, sometimes with a date ("November 3rd, 1917"), signed with the writer's ordinary name ("Pvt. Edward Whitfield," "Nurse A. Pemberton," "Mrs. Catherine Ashworth"). They are written in lowercase prose to someone the writer loves, in the careful tone of a person who knows a censor is reading over their shoulder. Letters often trail off mid-sentence, end without a closing, or contain one line that does not belong.

> The apple trees here remind me of the ones behind the house. I think about them more than I should. I think about

### Content

Observers sort 1917 material into four broad kinds, which apply to telegrams and letters alike:

- **Mundane.** Requests for socks, confirmation of rations, the weather. This material is completely incomprehensible to 3017 readers and is among the most-liked content on the platform.
- **Unsettling.** An otherwise ordinary message contains one line that should not be there: "THE BIRDS HAVE STOPPED STOP ALL OF THEM STOP"; "THE MEN IN THE TRENCH NEXT TO OURS ARE NOT OUR MEN STOP THEY LOOK LIKE OUR MEN STOP"; "The stars here are different than at home. There are too many of them and some of them are moving." The best known is **SOMETHING IS WRONG WITH THE SUN STOP**, after which the entire platform lost its mind. As of 3017, the forty-seven community notes attached to it all say UNKNOWN.
- **Accidentally beautiful.** A soldier writes home about the stars, the mud, a letter that arrived, or a dog he found. Some are just lists: "Bread. Clean water. Mother's Sunday roast. The dog. Silence." Nurses' letters mix clinical detail with small kindnesses: "We received fourteen more today. I have learned to sleep standing. The corporal in bed seven asked me to write to his wife and I said I would and I will."
- **Bureaucratic.** Supply requisitions, logistics chains, and form numbers from a pre-contact bureaucracy. See [Thrrm reaction](#reception) below.

### In reply threads

1917 fragments also surface inside ordinary reply threads, and when they do they tend to *obliquely echo the thread they land in*. A post about a late food delivery attracts a telegram about rations arriving late. A thread about a bad connection attracts a letter complaining that the telegraph has been behaving strangely. A thread about an irritating coworker attracts a soldier describing a man in the next trench who will not stop whistling. The writers plainly had no idea their words would appear under a post about orbital deli sandwiches eleven centuries later. Whether the echo is selection, coincidence, or something else is unknown.

### Reception

- **3017 users** react with bafflement, tenderness, or alarm: "new telegram just dropped"; "another one from the trenches"; "did. did a telegram just reply to my post about orbital deli sandwiches."; "the telegram is about socks. why am i crying about the socks."; "okay so i'm going to need someone to explain why a dead soldier just replied to my thread about soup."
- **The [Glœm](Gloem.md)** reply with devastating sincerity. They do not understand the content and perceive its emotional texture perfectly: "we have read this message. we do not know the sender. we do not know the war. we have invented a color for what this makes us feel. the color is not a happy one but it is important." The "socks telegram" in particular causes periodic Glœm weeping events.
- **The [Thrrm](Thrrm.md)** reply with procedural fury. 1917 requisitions cite form numbers from a deprecated pre-contact bureaucracy that complies with no ratified standard, and the originating bureau is in no registry, so no response can be filed. The Thrrm file complaints anyway. It is not clear against whom.
- **[Community notes](Community_Notes.md)** on 1917 material are appended in a tone of deadpan, professional bewilderment: "This transmission predates all known community context systems. The sender appears to reference a ground-based armed conflict (see: Galactic Military History Archive, Pre-Spaceflight section). Claims cannot be verified through standard processes. The Bureau recommends filing Form 91-Kappa (Temporal Anomaly Context Assessment) but acknowledges this form was not designed for pre-digital content." The surfaced ratings do not quite apply.

### Telegram voice

Some 3017 users post in "telegram voice" as a bit, with capitals, STOPs, and all the rest. Etiquette requires the imitator to break character at the end with a lowercase aside, so nobody mistakes the post for the real thing: "(i've been doing this bit for six months and nobody has told me to stop)", "(the keth love this one)", "(my therapist says this is a coping mechanism)". Imitations that omit the aside are reported quickly and resented for a long time.

## 2017

The 2017 anomalies come from the peak period of original [Twitter](Twitter.md), the era the modern Hellsite has spent a thousand years imitating. That is exactly why they are so disorienting: they are the source material, and nobody can read it.

2017 posts arrive in a **light-mode** presentation that most 3017 users have never seen: a white or pale grey background, blue accents, the old bird, round avatars, a flat blue checkmark where a sigil would be, and simple metrics (replies, reposts, likes). Display names are ordinary human names. The posts themselves are perfectly normal for their time and completely incomprehensible now.

- A post reading "uber driver just asked me if i listen to podcasts" was ratioed into oblivion by users who did not know what an uber, a driver, or a podcast was, and who were furious about all three.
- A 2017 post about being tired at work on a Monday received about two million likes after the Glœm declared it the most beautiful thing they had ever read. The Glœm have not explained. The original poster, presumably, never knew.
- The [Vrellnath](Vrellnath.md) find 2017 posts deeply moving for reasons they cannot articulate, which for the Vrellnath is unusual enough to be discussed in its own right.

**Community notes** on 2017 posts attempt earnest explanations, citing historical archives at length.

### The ancient note system

Some 2017 posts arrive **with their own notes attached**: period notes from the original context system. They are plain grey boxes headed *"Readers added context they thought people might want to know,"* rated on a single binary, **Helpful / Not Helpful**. They have no glyphs, no rating dimensions, and no Thrrm compliance. The 3017 audience finds this simplicity *deeply unsettling*. A fact-check system with only two rating options reads, in the words of one widely shared post, "like a legal document from a civilization that hadn't invented nuance yet."

A 2017 post frequently collects a **modern note stacked on top of its ancient one**, in which the 3017 system tries to explain, from the archives, what the original post and its original note meant.

### Advertising

Promoted posts sometimes arrive with the anomalies. The best-known 2017 examples advertise a mattress company (the name most often cited is Casper). Nobody in 3017 knows what a mattress is. The ads are enthusiastic about it anyway.

## 2517

The 2517 anomalies come from the "middle period," the stretch of history that nobody on the platform remembers well. Most of that era's archives were lost in the **Great Data Collapse of 2623**, so the events and people that 2517 posts refer to cannot be looked up. What survives is the posts.

2517 memes are *almost* recognizable. The formats are nearly the formats and the jokes are nearly jokes, but everything is slightly off in a way many users find unsettling. They read like someone doing an impression of humor in a language they almost speak.

2517 posts are rendered in a **sepia-shifted** version of the modern look, with a **deprecated glyph set** and metrics in units nobody uses anymore:

> 4.2 resonances · 890 echoes · 12 folds

Nobody is sure what a fold was.

The Thrrm are furious about 2517 content. It references forms from deprecated systems that are too recent to ignore and too old to process.

### Middle-period notes

2517 posts sometimes carry notes from the context system of their own time. It used an intermediate rating scheme of roughly eight to ten dimensions. Some map onto current dimensions, and others are unrecognizable and cannot be reconstructed.

### Advertising

2517 promoted posts advertise products that no longer exist, with genuinely alarming side effects that the ads treat as routine.

## Period advertising in general

Each era's anomalies occasionally bring that era's advertising with them. The 1917 equivalent is reported to be **war bonds posters**. The 3017 audience has no framework for these at all.[citation needed]

## Cultural references

- "new telegram just dropped" is a stock phrase, now also used ironically for any old news.
- "send socks" is shorthand for a small, desperate, reasonable request.
- "something is wrong with the sun" is used to mean a detail that should not be in an otherwise ordinary post. It is rarely said lightly.
- "light-mode energy" means content that is sincere, unbothered, and incomprehensible.
- "measured in folds" describes a metric nobody can interpret.
