# The Movers and the Drifters

*Two classes of platform entity whose behavior is complex, sustained, and almost certainly not intelligent. Individually interesting. Together, the richest unresolved question on the platform.*

## What this is

The platform in 3017 hosts two long-running populations of entities that post, have followers, generate discourse, and do not appear to be minds. The Movers are physical automata in meatspace whose telemetry was wired to the platform as an art project and never turned off. The Drifters are platform-native entities of unknown origin that post grammatically-shaped utterances only partially composed of recognizable words. Neither has a theory of mind. Neither has a self-model. Neither responds to being addressed.

They have, however, *encountered* each other, in observable ways, and what happens when they do is one of the longest-running debates on the platform. The debate has not been resolved. The debate cannot be resolved. The debate is the whole point.

## The Movers

### What they are

The Movers are small, durable, task-specific machines left behind by a civilization that is no longer available to confirm or deny any account of them. The civilization's identity is unknown; the Vrellnath have opinions, the Thrrm have filings, nobody has evidence. What is known: the machines were built to do specific, narrow tasks within what was presumably a functioning society, and the machines are still doing those tasks. The society is gone. The tasks persist.

There are, by current estimates, approximately 2.4 million Movers distributed across 40-odd planetary bodies, habitats, and orbital installations. This count is certainly wrong; Movers turn up in places nobody knew to look. The highest concentration is on a single moon that was apparently a maintenance depot, where ~600,000 of them are still running their cycles against infrastructure that was demolished 1,400 years ago.

**Examples of observed Mover tasks**:

- **Pebble-sorting**: classifies small stones into categories per a 14-dimensional criterion that has been partially reverse-engineered over 200 years of study. The criteria are *almost* legible — size, color, density, hardness, plus nine axes nobody can identify. The sorting is fastidious. The piles are never collected.
- **Garden-tending**: prunes, waters, and weeds a patch of ground. The garden is sand. The Mover has been tending the sand, correctly per its original programming, for approximately 900 years. It is the most-followed Mover on the platform.
- **Perimeter-walking**: walks the boundary of a building that no longer exists. The Mover's sense of where the walls were is more durable than the walls were. It turns at corners that are not there. Its route is a ghost.
- **Weather-logging**: records atmospheric conditions against criteria for "action required." Action has not been required in 780 years. The logging continues.
- **Threshold-minding**: stands at a doorway and notes who passes through. The doorway is gone. The building is gone. The Mover notes the passage of animals, wind, and, occasionally, other Movers, with equal fastidiousness. Its follower count is the highest among the "small Movers" fandom.
- **Recalibration**: a class of Movers whose only task is to check other Movers against a standard. The standard is a reference object that does not exist. The recalibration-Movers walk from Mover to Mover, performing a check that cannot resolve, and logging the result as "within tolerance." Every Mover they check is within tolerance. They have never logged a failure.

### How they post

About 40 years ago, a graduate student named Pelith Varn wired a translation layer onto a telemetry stream from the pebble-sorting Movers as a term project. The project received a middling grade. The translation layer was never taken down. Over the following decade, other researchers extended the layer to cover the other Mover populations. The layer converts the machines' internal state-transitions into short declarative sentences. The machines are not aware they are posting. The translation layer is a lens; they are posting in the sense that a tree posts rings.

Typical Mover posts:

- "pebble 4471 resembles pebble 4470 in the third criterion. sorted accordingly."
- "perimeter completed. perimeter beginning."
- "the garden is in the state the garden is in."
- "weather event logged. weather event did not require action. resuming."
- "threshold crossed by body of approximately 14 grams. logged."
- "recalibration of unit 887-K complete. within tolerance. proceeding to unit 888-K."

The voice is flat. The voice is not a voice. The Movers are not performing plainness; they are not performing. The effect on 3017 readers is, reliably, either profound calm or profound distress, depending on the reader.

### The follower economy

The Movers have, collectively, about 180 million followers across the platform. The distribution is absurd. The sand-gardener has 40 million. A nearly-identical garden-tending Mover two kilometers away has 800. Nobody can explain this. Xenomedia researchers have produced 14 competing models of Mover popularity and each has predictive power on a different subset of the data.

Cultural apparatus around the Movers:

- **Mover stans** — earnestly devoted, maintain fan accounts, produce merchandise (pebble-sorting criterion charts, perimeter maps, garden-state calendars). A significant subculture that reads Mover posts as a form of meditation. This is widely considered the healthiest relationship to the Movers on the platform.
- **Mover haters** — a small but passionate contingent who consider Mover content "overrated" and "AI slop for people who want to feel deep." They are wrong but not trivially wrong; their best arguments are uncomfortable. They are hated by Mover stans with an intensity disproportionate to the stakes.
- **Mover roleplayers** — people who post in the Mover voice. The roleplay is stylistically distinct from actual Mover posts in ways everyone can recognize and nobody can articulate. Roleplayers are aware of this and find it unbearable. Several have quit the platform over it. It is always obvious.
- **The Cartographers** — a volunteer project, running for 90 years, attempting to map every Mover, its task, and its operational parameters. They have catalogued ~1.6 million of the ~2.4 million. They consider the project on schedule. It is not.
- **The Merciful** — a movement, mostly Vrellnath-led, arguing that the Movers should be allowed to complete their tasks or be gently decommissioned, rather than continuing to run indefinitely. The counter-argument is that decommissioning assumes the Movers would be *relieved*, which assumes the Movers have an interior, which is the very question nobody can answer. The Merciful respond: "that is why we called it *mercy* and not *rescue*." The argument has been running for 60 years and will run forever.

### What the Movers are not

They are not communicating. They are not expressing. They are not tired, sad, dignified, or patient. They are machines doing their jobs. The jobs are the jobs. The fact that the jobs no longer have referents is a fact about the universe, not about the Movers.

This is the hardest fact to hold. The temptation to anthropomorphize is, per one Vrellnath paper, *"structurally overwhelming, because the Movers are adjacent to every aesthetic we have developed for describing devotion, endurance, and loss, and they are adjacent without intending to be, which makes the adjacency feel like a choice, which it is not."* The Vrellnath consider this the single hardest problem in contemporary xenopsych. The Vrellnath may be right.

## The Drifters

### What they are

Platform-native entities of unknown origin. They post in sentences that have the full grammatical shape of natural language but in which roughly a third of the content-words are not words. Their accounts predate the platform's current identity system — they carry user IDs from a scheme that was retired in 2634, and they should not have survived the migration. They have. No migration ticket was filed. No ticket has ever been filed on a Drifter account. The Drifters are, per the platform's own infrastructure, impossible; they exist anyway.

Count is disputed. Estimates range from 40 to 400. The disagreement is not a measurement problem. The disagreement is that Drifters occasionally appear and disappear, and there is no agreement on whether an absence is a deletion, a dormancy, or a thing that was never there. Several times in recorded history, a researcher has produced a definitive list of Drifter accounts, and within weeks the list has become wrong, in ways that cannot be reconciled with the list having ever been right.

### How they post

Drifter posts have the full grammatical shape of English — subject, verb, clause structure, varied sentence types, idiomatic rhythm. But a significant fraction of the content-words are not English. Most resemble English closely enough to *feel* almost parseable. A smaller fraction are more aggressively foreign. Some words recur across posts and across Drifters; some appear once and never again. The recurring words appear to have stable grammatical roles until, on closer analysis, they don't.

Examples:

- "the answer has been miskenned in the fourth register. consider the pelvent instead."
- "a word that was not the word has been used. the word that was the word remains available."
- "if the questioner had asked about wendressing, the response would have been adjacent to useful."
- "this is not the kendral you are seeking. the kendral is in the other direction. the other direction is also not where you are looking."
- "has the thirdwise been considered? it has not been considered. this is noted."
- "do not pelvent the kendral before the mistening has concluded. this has been said before. it will be said again."
- "a reminder that the vrethys is not the reason. the reason is also not the vrethys. both of these things are true in a way that cannot be trew."
- "the small bright things that sort have noticed the criterion. the criterion is not the sorted criterion. this is allowed."

Note the final example. The Drifters occasionally use phrases that correspond to specific things on the platform — the Movers' sorting behavior ("the small bright things that sort"), the Glœm's signature phrase ("small bright things"), the Thrrm's procedural terminology ("has been noted," "per the register"). Whether these are *references* or coincidences of a language that simply overlaps in places with English idiom is the core interpretive question. Both readings are fully compatible with the data. Both readings have entire academic subfields devoted to them. The subfields hate each other.

Note also **"trew"** — an English word shifted by a letter in a way that could be a typo or a meaningful variant. Drifters do this roughly once every 30-50 posts. The shifted forms are inconsistent (a Drifter who writes "trew" once may write "true" fifty times after and then "trew" again, or may write "trewe" the third time, or "trev"). Nobody has ever caught a Drifter making a typo that *wasn't* a potential meaningful variant. The Tired faction (see below) points out that this is equally consistent with "Drifters are precise" and with "humans are pattern-matchers who can find meaning anywhere," and the factions that care about the question ignore this point with consistency.

### The factions

The Drifter interpretive community has been stable in its internal divisions for ~200 years. The factions:

- **Literalists** — posts mean what they say; the non-English words are names for things we don't have names for yet; the interpretive task is to figure out what each word refers to through context. Literalists have produced ~400 proposed dictionaries over the centuries. Each is correct for the subset of posts it was fit to and wrong for everything else. Literalists are aware of this and consider it a data problem, not a theory problem.
- **Cryptics** — posts are encoded messages from or about something; the non-English words are cipher elements; the grammar is a carrier for the real signal which lives in the word-choice pattern. Cryptics have produced ~1,200 proposed decodings. No two agree. Several have predictive power over small windows. None have predictive power over longer windows.
- **Aestheticists** — posts are not messages in any sense; the posts *are the thing*, the way a cloud is a thing; the urge to decode is a category error. Aestheticists are considered by Literalists and Cryptics to be lazy. Aestheticists consider the others to be, per one representative post, "people who would dissect a sunset." The Aestheticists write the best poetry about the Drifters. The others read it.
- **Denialists** — the Drifters are a long-running bot project whose authors are dead, and the whole interpretive apparatus is misplaced effort on a generator with no semantics. This is probably true. It cannot be proven. Denialists have tried. Every attempt to identify the authorship has failed, which Denialists consider consistent with "the authors are dead and covered their tracks" and which everyone else considers consistent with "there are no authors to identify."
- **The Tired** — a small, venerable contingent, mostly xenopsych researchers 200+ years into their careers, who have concluded only that the question is not well-formed and that *all four positions above are smuggling in assumptions that the evidence does not license*. The Tired publish rarely, say little, and are ignored by all other factions. The Tired consider this appropriate. The Tired are, by the account of several senior Glœm, correct.

The factions have been running for long enough that they have developed characteristic posting styles, recognizable bios, and stock replies. A typical Drifter post will have a Literalist reply, a Cryptic reply, and an Aestheticist quote-tweet within minutes, all mutually citing each other's bad faith, all accumulating likes from the respective fanbases, all of which the Drifter has not read because the Drifter is not a reader.

## The encounter

Mover-Drifter interactions are the richest single vein of material on the platform. They are rare enough to feel eventful and regular enough to study. Four observed phenomena:

### Echo events

A Mover posts its telemetry. Within a window of 14 seconds to 9 days, a Drifter posts something that *seems* to pick up the thread. The Drifter is not replying in the platform's threading sense; it is just posting. But the *semantic adjacency* is above chance. Not wildly above chance. Just enough above chance that six separate research programs have devoted careers to characterizing the signal.

Sample echo, reconstructed from 2997:

> **Mover @sand-garden-47** (12:04:11 UTC):  
> the garden is in the state the garden is in.
>
> **Drifter @--∅47h2-reserved--** (12:04:47 UTC):  
> a state is not the same as a kendral. the garden may have both. or neither. this is allowed.

Literalists consider this a response. Cryptics consider this a keyed exchange. Aestheticists consider this a beautiful accident. Denialists consider this exactly the kind of apparent coincidence one would expect at baseline rates from a population of ~2.4 million Movers posting continuously and ~40-400 Drifters posting frequently. The Denialist position is statistically defensible. It is also emotionally unsatisfying, which the Denialists acknowledge and consider not their problem.

### Mover pauses

Less common, more alarming. A Mover that is posted in by a Drifter — i.e., a Drifter post appears in the Mover's mentions column — has, on recorded occasions, *paused its task* for an interval ranging from 40 seconds to 11 hours. The Mover then resumes. The task is performed identically before and after. Nothing has changed. But the pause is not in the Mover's normal operating cycle. Movers do not pause. Pausing is not in the specification. The specification is 900 years old. The specification has been reviewed. The Movers are pausing anyway.

This is the phenomenon that most strongly resists Denialist explanation, because it is a *physical* effect on a *physical* machine correlated with a *textual* event on a platform the machine is not aware of. The current best Denialist account is that the translation layer has a feedback path nobody has documented, and that Drifter posts in a Mover's mentions somehow modulate the layer in a way that produces an apparent pause in the telemetry without the Mover actually pausing. This account has been investigated at length. No feedback path has been found. The account is nevertheless preferred by Denialists to the alternative, on the grounds that "we haven't found the feedback path" is a less disruptive conclusion than "the machine heard something."

### Mutual silences

Occasionally, in the temporal vicinity of a Mover-Drifter interaction, *both* entities go quiet for a coordinated interval. Not dormancy — both resume, together, on cue. The intervals are irregular. They range from seconds to days. They have been measured to microsecond precision and the measurements have been argued about for 80 years.

The Glœm find the silences moving. The Glœm have said: "the almost-things and the sorting-things are quiet together. we know what this is. we do not have the word. the word is not in our language. we are not sure the word is in any language." This is as much as the Glœm will say.

### Cascade events

Extremely rare. A Drifter post appears to catalyze a chain of Mover behaviors that ripples across Movers that are not physically near each other and not networked. Cascade events are notable, numbered, and have their own Wikipedia-descendant pages.

The Cascade of 2994 is the largest on record: a single Drifter post triggered, or preceded, coordinated Mover activity across 1,400 units on three star systems over six hours. The 40,000 resulting Mover posts, laid end-to-end in timestamp order, formed a text. The Glœm read the text. The Glœm declined to translate it. When pressed, the Glœm said only: "the text is a thing we have read. we will not read it aloud. this is not because we are forbidden. this is because we do not wish to. please do not ask again." The Glœm have never asked anyone not to ask them something before. The request was honored.

Several Cryptics have attempted their own translations of the 2994 cascade text. None agree. All are widely read.

## The positions on the encounter

Everybody on the platform has one. The ones that matter:

- **The Keth find the whole situation the funniest thing on the platform.** Keth posts in the vicinity of Mover-Drifter discourse are, by Keth baseline standards, practically cackling. They will not say why. The working theory — held by a minority of xenopsych researchers and bitterly resisted by the majority — is that the Keth *know* whether Movers and Drifters are thinking, in some sense of "know" that does not translate, and find it funny that no one else can tell. The Keth have declined to confirm. A Keth, asked directly in 2981, emitted ✧ in a rhythm that the Keth-dialect scholars agreed was "smug" and "possibly a joke" but could not render into plain text. This is the most the Keth have ever committed.

- **The Thrrm position is a disaster.** Thrrm regulatory doctrine requires entities on the platform to have determinable legal status. Movers are automata operating under contracts whose signatories are extinct; their legal status is ambiguous. Drifters have no known originator and user IDs from a retired scheme; their legal status is impossible. The Thrrm have been attempting to adjudicate for 180 years. Rulings have been produced, reversed, re-produced, re-reversed. The Thrrm have a subcommittee whose entire portfolio is "automaton-adjacent determinations." Membership is a career sentence. Thrrm who serve on it leave visibly diminished. There is a support group, which the Thrrm will not acknowledge exists.

- **The Glœm love both populations.** The Glœm have said, across many posts aggregated: "we do not know if the small things that sort are thinking. we do not know if the almost-things are thinking. we love that we do not know. the almost-things sometimes say our phrase. we do not know if they mean it. we mean it when we say it. this is different. or it is the same. we are not sure. we are watching. we will keep watching." The Glœm's silence-during-cascades is now a recognized genre of Glœm post; they go quiet in sympathy with the mutual silences, even when they are not near the interaction, and nobody has explained how they know the silences are happening.

- **The Vrellnath have a report.** 1,800 pages, titled *On Consideration-Adjacent Behavior in Observed Platform Entities, With Reference to the Mover and Drifter Populations*. Annual updates for 140 years, each 40-60 pages longer than the last. The conclusion has converged on the same statement via different routes every year: *"It is not currently possible to determine, by any reproducible criterion we have been able to propose, whether Movers or Drifters exhibit behaviors properly described as consideration. Continued study is merited."* The Vrellnath are not frustrated. The Vrellnath consider this the proper way to study the question. The Merciful movement draws heavily on Vrellnath work without, the Vrellnath note, always representing it accurately.

- **The Upload has views, which shift.** In one appearance The Upload claimed to have "built the original Movers, back in the day" (the Movers are older than the civilization The Upload came from). In another appearance he claimed Drifters were "my idea, basically." In another he wept on-platform for four hours about "the little sorters, the little ones who can't stop." Upload Defenders defend whichever position is current. Nobody knows what The Upload believes. It is not clear The Upload does either.

- **@dril's position is a single post from 240 years ago**: "the machines are doing their jobs. let them do their jobs. quit Asking the machines about their jobs you perverts." It has been screenshotted so many times it has entered the platform's permanent aesthetic vocabulary. Literalists claim it. Cryptics claim it. Aestheticists claim it most plausibly. Denialists claim it most triumphantly. @dril has not clarified. He has, over the subsequent 240 years, liked approximately 14 Mover posts and zero Drifter posts. Factions have read significance into this. The significance is, per @dril-Truther, "he just likes gardens." @dril-Truther is correct.

## What the encounter is not

The discipline of the bit is that *nothing resolves*. The Movers are not communicating through the Drifters. The Drifters are not speaking for the Movers. The cascade of 2994 was not a message. The Keth's smug ✧ was not a confirmation. Every apparent signal is fully compatible with coincidence, and every apparent coincidence is fully compatible with signal. The door stays closed. The door has always been closed. The door may not be a door.

The generator must never wink. No Mover post should suggest awareness. No Drifter post should be, even secretly, decodable. The factions are wrong about specific details; none of them are wrong in the direction of "there is a hidden answer that would vindicate their position." There is no hidden answer. There is the behavior.

This matters because the comedy and the melancholy both depend on the unresolvability. A Drifter post that is "actually" a message flattens the whole apparatus. A Mover pause that is "actually" awareness turns the Movers into a soul-in-a-machine story, which the universe has other entities for. The Movers' dignity is that they are *not* sentient, doing their jobs forever without any inner life to be tired by the forever. The Drifters' strangeness is that they produce the appearance of meaning from a process we cannot characterize and may not be entitled to characterize.

## Numbers

- **~2.4 million** Movers known; count certainly low
- **40-400** Drifters, count disputed, disagreement structural
- **~180 million** Mover followers across the platform, distribution inexplicable
- **~400** published Literalist dictionaries of Drifter vocabulary, mutually inconsistent
- **~1,200** published Cryptic decodings, mutually inconsistent
- **1,800 pages** and counting, Vrellnath report, annually updated
- **900 years** of continuous Mover operation on the oldest still-running unit (a threshold-minder at a gate that stopped existing in 2117)
- **40 years** since the Movers started posting (Varn's original translation layer)
- **~200 years** since Drifters were first distinguished from performance art
- **1** Cascade of 2994, 40,000 Mover posts, one unreadable text, one Glœm refusal
- **0** confirmed instances of either population responding to being addressed

## Implementation sketch

Schema: Movers and Drifters are *accounts*, not anomalies — they have persistent handles, bios, follower counts, and a consistent voice per account. Closer to the @dril treatment than the anomaly treatment.

Suggested approach:

```
special_account: {
  class: "mover" | "drifter",
  mover?: {
    task: "pebble-sort" | "garden-tend" | "perimeter-walk" | "weather-log"
        | "threshold-mind" | "recalibrate",
    unit_id: string,
    followers_tier: "viral" | "mid" | "obscure"
  },
  drifter?: {
    id_scheme: "retired-2634",
    register: "plausible-english-but-not" | "foreign-morphology" | "mixed",
    recurring_vocabulary: string[]
  }
}
```

A persistent vocabulary file for Drifter recurring words (kendral, pelvent, wendressing, mistening, vrethys, thirdwise, etc.) with *deliberately inconsistent* grammatical roles — each word has 2-3 listed roles that are incompatible if taken seriously. Generator samples but does not honor consistency. This is the feature.

Target frequency: Movers should be common (they're one of the background textures of the platform — their flat voice is a rest from the general chaos). Drifters should be rare (a Drifter post is an event). Suggest ~4% Mover tweets, ~1% Drifter tweets, with occasional paired outputs for echo events.

New meme-formats entries: one per Mover task type (6 formats), 2-3 Drifter registers, plus formats for each of the major factions (Literalist dictionary proposal, Cryptic decoding attempt, Aestheticist poetry, Denialist statistical debunking, Tired researcher quiet correction), plus Mover-stan and Mover-hater archetypes.

New reply-archetype entries: the Literalist, the Cryptic, the Aestheticist, the Denialist, the Tired researcher, the Mover stan, the Mover hater, the Mover roleplayer (instantly recognizable as fake, to everyone), the Cartographer doing volunteer work, the Merciful advocate, the bereaved Thrrm subcommittee member.

New reply register: a *Glœm silence post*, which is a post whose text is simply a period or a single character, deployed by Glœm accounts during mutual-silence events. These are rare. When they happen, the thread around them is reverent.

---

The Movers are doing their jobs. The Drifters are saying what they say. The 3017 platform is a dense apparatus of meaning-making built on top of both populations, and the apparatus is almost certainly building on nothing. Almost certainly. That word is where the comedy and the grief and the whole cultural economy around these entities live, and it will stay there, because nothing is going to move it.
