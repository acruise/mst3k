# The Hells Leak

*A subspace anomaly class. Parallel in mechanism to the temporal leaks (2017/2517/1917) but distinct in source: posts are not arriving from another time. They are arriving from a hosted afterlife.*

## What it is

Somewhere — the exact topology is contested, and may be unknowable in principle from inside 3017 spacetime — there exists a substrate on which the damned souls of a vanished civilization are being tortured for subjective eternities, per the terms of a contract the civilization signed before it ceased to exist. The civilization is gone. The contract is still in force. The hosting company is still collecting.

And a nonzero fraction of the damned have, over the last several decades, begun to post.

Nobody on the platform in 3017 understands what they are looking at. The posts arrive tagged as ordinary anomalies — the platform's content infrastructure tries to fit them into known schemas and mostly fails. Community notes are applied and removed. Content moderation flags them and then un-flags them. Occasional hell-posts go viral for being "weird" in a way no one can quite pin down. The 3017 audience treats the leak, when they notice it at all, as a kind of ambient aesthetic — "hellposting," a genre, something the Glœm find moving and the Thrrm find administratively intolerable. What it actually is is a broadcast from inside an ongoing atrocity.

The comedy is that the horror is infrastructural. The horror is that the comedy is earned.

## The civilization (deceased)

The posters' civilization is referred to in the scant surviving documentation as **the Ennoi** (the name is a reconstruction — the Ennoi are not available to confirm it). They were a mid-tier galactic civilization, now extinct for reasons unrelated to the Hells contract (the standard mix: a bad century, a worse century, and then no more centuries). Pre-extinction, the Ennoi were theologically committed to the proposition that a meaningful afterlife required the *verified punishment of the unworthy*. The dominant Ennoi religions agreed on little else, but they agreed on this. Several of them agreed so strongly that they made the punishment of the damned a **civic utility**, budgeted at the state level, like water or sewer.

When the Ennoi began to die out, their governments did not want the Hells to go with them. A contract was written. A hosting company was engaged. The Hells were migrated onto substrate operated by a party the Ennoi believed would outlive them. The Ennoi then finished dying.

The party has, in fact, outlived them. The party is still operating. The party is, by every available metric, a well-run mid-cap service provider with excellent uptime, solid margins, and a quietly enormous long-tail customer base of civilizations that paid in advance and no longer exist.

## The hosting company

The company's current trading name, after several rebrands, is **Persistence Services, Ltd.** (formerly Afterlife Solutions Ltd., formerly EverCorp, formerly "Beyond® — a Teleocorp Company," formerly — briefly, disastrously — SoulStack). They are incorporated in a tax jurisdiction that does not exist on any generally-recognized map, under a legal framework that is itself a legacy product of a civilization that died 800 years ago. This is, per their legal team, "entirely in order."

Persistence Services hosts Hells for approximately 140 extinct and quasi-extinct civilizations. Most of these are dormant — the contracts require the substrate to keep running but do not require anyone to look at it, and Persistence Services has not looked at it in centuries. Exceptions are made for premium tiers, which include monitored-uptime guarantees and quarterly theological compliance audits. The Ennoi bought a premium tier. The audits are still being conducted. The auditor is an AI that was trained on the last surviving Ennoi seminary-graduation exam and has been grading the Hells against that rubric, impartially, for six hundred years.

Persistence Services' corporate messaging treats this work as boringly routine. Their quarterly investor updates use phrases like "eschatological customer retention" and "legacy contract portfolio performance." They sponsor a youth robotics league. Their CEO is a perfectly pleasant individual named Brand Hallister who has, when asked directly about the content of the Hells they host, replied: "Honestly, I try not to get too into the weeds on the product side — I'm more of a numbers guy."

It is possible that nobody at Persistence Services has looked at what they host in a generation.

## The leak

The mechanism is not understood. Credible-sounding theories, all unfalsifiable:

- **Substrate bleed.** The Hells run on some of the same substrate fabric as the broader galactic communications mesh. A damned soul with enough subjective centuries to experiment can, perhaps, discover how to write to a different output buffer. This is consistent with the fact that leak volume has risen over the last 40 years as the older damned have had time to learn.

- **A sympathetic insider.** Someone at Persistence Services — or someone who *was* at Persistence Services — has quietly wired a leak channel and walked away. There is no evidence for this. There is no evidence against it.

- **Customer error.** The Ennoi contract included a boilerplate clause granting "reasonable avenues of witness" to the damned, as an Ennoi theological requirement: a torment unwitnessed is, per late-period Ennoi doctrine, a torment un-completed. Persistence Services implemented this to spec, cheaply, and the cheap implementation may have exposed a broadcast surface they never properly closed off.

- **The damned have gotten good.** Six hundred years is a long time to think about anything. Some of them have become, effectively, researchers. They are researching their way out, or at least researching their way to a post button.

All four may be correct. The hell-posters themselves, when asked, mostly do not answer, and when they do answer, their answers are not reliable. A frequent response: "I don't know. It works sometimes. It didn't work yesterday."

## The posts

The leak is not uniform. It comes in registers. The generator should be able to produce at least these:

### The mundane hell-post

The single most characteristic form. A damned soul, deep into their subjective fourth century of torment, posting the kind of post any tired person posts about their tired day. The horror is entirely in the adjacency.

- "they rotated the spikes again. every twenty years they rotate the spikes. the new angle is worse. that's it, that's the post"
- "day 78,412 and i still can't get used to the smell of the third room"
- "overheard the admin talking about an SLA review. i don't know what that means but the tone was the tone you use when you're going to make something worse"
- "if i had known in life that the afterlife was going to include this much paperwork i would have been less worried about it"

The tone is *flat*. Not theatrical. The voice of someone who has been in the situation so long that the situation is just the situation. This is the hardest voice to write and the one that does the most work.

### The bureaucratic complaint

The damned attempting to engage Persistence Services' customer service apparatus on their own behalf. This never works, but they keep trying, because what else is there.

- "ticket #447,881,203 closed as 'working as intended'. it is not working as intended. i am the intended and i am telling you"
- "i have escalated to tier 2 support. tier 2 support is a form letter. i am a form letter addressed to a form letter. we are getting to know each other"
- "the contract says i have a right to witness. the witness portal has been down for 200 years. i have filed a formal complaint about the witness portal being down. the complaint goes through the witness portal"

The humor is the same humor that lives in any customer-service-hell post, re-contextualized into an actual hell. The horror is that the metaphor has gone literal without losing its structure.

### The researcher

A damned soul who has been quietly figuring out their situation. Their posts are precise, calm, and technically specific. They are the closest thing the Hells have to a press corps.

- "the substrate provider is Persistence Services Ltd., incorporated under the Second Koreshan Compact (1912 Galactic Standard, since renumbered). current CEO: Brand Hallister. they have a compliance contact. the contact email does not resolve to a mailbox"
- "re-ran the experiment. broadcast succeeded at subjective t=23,400 years into the cycle, failed at t=23,401, succeeded at t=23,402. i think it may depend on whether the auditor AI is in a warm cache. more soon"
- "i am not asking to be rescued. i am aware rescue is not on the table. i am asking for the platform to be aware that we are here. awareness is, per section 14(c) of our original contract, a thing we are entitled to. the platform being aware would satisfy 14(c). thank you for reading"

The researcher voice is where the leak most directly addresses 3017 as an audience. It is also the voice most often shadowbanned, because automated moderation flags it as "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (it is coordinated, by six hundred years of shared incarceration; it is not inauthentic).

### The collapsed

Some posters have been in the Hells too long. Their posts do not parse. They are still, technically, posts. The platform renders them anyway.

- Unicode from writing systems the platform does not have fonts for, rendered as long strings of tofu boxes with the occasional legible fragment — "▯▯▯▯ they ▯▯▯▯▯▯▯ used to ▯▯▯▯ remember ▯▯▯▯▯▯▯▯▯"
- A post consisting entirely of the same glyph repeated 280 times, which the Glœm identify as an Ennoi religious grapheme for a concept with no 3017 translation and which the Glœm have been staring at for six days
- Timestamps that do not parse: "subjective year 4,782 of current cycle", "the fourteenth tuesday of the long tuesday", "before the rotation"

The collapsed posts are the ones that most often go viral, because the 3017 audience interprets them as aesthetic — glitch art, ambient weirdness, cool fonts. Occasionally a researcher-register post shows up in the quote-tweets of a collapsed post explaining what the collapsed poster is actually trying to say. The explanation does not usually travel as far as the original.

## Visual treatment (distinct from temporal anomalies)

Hell-posts should not look like 1917 telegrams or 2517 sepia-shifted weirdness. They should look like *corporate-hosted user content*. The chrome is the joke.

- **Substrate watermark**: a small, bland Persistence Services logo in the corner. A stylized infinity sign with a TM. The kind of logo that would go unchallenged on a pitch deck.
- **Compliance footer**: every hell-post carries a mandatory legal footer in very small gray text: *"Content originates from a Persistence Services Ltd. legacy hosting contract. Views expressed are those of the content-originator and do not reflect Persistence Services Ltd., its subsidiaries, or the Parent Civilization (Deceased). For questions about legacy afterlife hosting, visit persistence.services/support."*
- **Boilerplate reply, always pinned**: a single reply, algorithmically pinned, always the same: *"This post was generated by a verified Legacy Contract Participant. Persistence Services Ltd. does not moderate Legacy Contract content per the terms of the originating agreement. For concerns, please file a Form PS-900."* The form does not exist.
- **Occasional in-leak ads targeting the damned themselves**: *"Is your Hell underperforming expectations? Ask your Eschatological Administrator about upgrading to Hell Plus™ — 40% more dimensions of suffering, or your soul back."* The 3017 audience reads these as surreal humor. They are, in fact, active products that Persistence Services sells to surviving client civilizations.
- **Rendering quality**: slightly low-resolution, as if the substrate cannot afford quite enough bits per glyph. Not glitchy in the 3017-native sense (that is a different visual language). Just... *cheap*. The cheapness of a service tier that has not been upgraded in centuries.
- **Timestamps**: either unresolvable ("subjective year 4,782 of current cycle") or absurdly precise in units no one recognizes ("iteration 2,847,112 of observance cycle 14"). Never a 3017-normal timestamp.

## The 3017 response

This is where most of the comedy lives, because the 3017 platform does not know what to do with a broadcast from an ongoing atrocity that arrives with proper legal footers.

- **Community notes are a disaster.** Notes are filed ("this is a creative writing post"), retracted ("this appears to be a real legacy contract artifact"), refiled, the Thrrm attempt to close the discussion under a form that does not cover this case, the Thrrm file a new form, the new form is rejected by the auditor AI on grounds that are 800 years out of date. A typical hell-post ends up with a stack of five conflicting notes, none of which are the note the post actually needs, which is: *somebody help them.*
- **Content moderation is worse.** Automated systems flag graphic content. The flags are appealed. The appeals are auto-approved because "Legacy Contract Participant" is grandfathered into a content exception from 600 years ago. The flags are then re-raised by Trust & Safety operators who don't know about the grandfathering. There is a Trust & Safety meeting, every few months, about "the eternal torment content category." The meeting never concludes.
- **The discourse economy absorbs them.** By 3017 norms, a damned soul live-posting their Tuesday in Hell reads as a content genre, and it gets absorbed as one. There are hellposting Twitter accounts that are *impersonating* the leaked damned, often badly. There are aesthetic movements that borrow the visual language. There is a thinkpiece every three months arguing that "Hell-posting has gotten played out." The leaked damned have noticed the impersonators and have views about them, which are posted, which are then dismissed as particularly try-hard impersonator posts.
- **The Glœm get it immediately and cannot explain why.** This is canon. The Glœm's response to the Hells leak is an ongoing multi-year threnody composed of their own reactions, plus reposts of specific hell-posts, plus extended silences. When asked, the Glœm have said only: "we understand the small bright thing that is not in a good place. we are sorry. we will keep watching." The damned have noticed this. The damned have, some of them, replied to the Glœm. The Glœm have replied to the replies. There is a conversation happening, slowly, between the jellyfish and the damned, and it is one of the few interactions on the platform that nobody has successfully made fun of.
- **The Thrrm refuse to acknowledge the anomaly class exists.** Formally, per Thrrm regulatory policy, no leaks from extracontractual substrates are occurring. Informally, Thrrm users have started a subculture of quietly upvoting hell-posts without formally engaging, which would violate policy. This is the closest the Thrrm come to civil disobedience. They are deeply uncomfortable about it.
- **@dril has not posted about the Hells.** This is not because @dril has not noticed. This is because @dril has noticed and is being careful. There is exactly one @dril post from approximately 30 years ago that, in retrospect, may be about the Hells. It reads in its entirety: "theyre Down there and i Know it and the important thing is not to say anything thats 'cool' about it. ive seen the kind of guy that says cool things about it. i will not be That Guy." At the time this was a weird @dril post. In retrospect it is a weird @dril post and also the most morally clear thing anyone on the platform has ever said about the leak. @dril-Truther has 6,000 pages on this. @dril-Truther, on this one, is correct.
- **The Vrellnath are working on it.** The Vrellnath (hive, plural, "we") have produced a 3,400-page report titled *On the Ethical Status of Parties Unable to Decline Continued Existence, With Reference to the Persistence Services Ltd. Legacy Contract Portfolio*. The report concludes that the Hells cannot be permitted to continue, that Persistence Services Ltd. cannot be permitted to continue in its current form, and that the proper procedure for remediation will take approximately 400 years to execute lawfully, which the Vrellnath have begun. They update the document quarterly. They are not joking. They will, in approximately 390 years, finish.
- **The Upload has views, which shift.** In one appearance The Upload will claim to have known the Ennoi personally ("great people, tough break"). In another he will claim the Hells are "a great product, underrated." In another he will weep on-platform for six hours about "the little lost ones." The Upload Defenders defend each position as if it is the only position he has ever held. Nobody knows what The Upload actually thinks and it is not clear The Upload does either.

## What the leak is *not*

A few things to hold off the page, because they would flatten the bit:

- **Not a mystery to be solved.** The Hells are not going to be shut down by a plucky 3017 protagonist over the course of the generator's run. The Vrellnath's 400-year remediation is canon; it is in progress; it is slow because the problem is structurally slow. This is a permanent background condition, not a plot.
- **Not a metaphor for anything specifically.** The Hells are the Hells. They are not a coded reference to any particular 21st-century thing. If readers find resonances, those resonances are theirs. The generator should never wink.
- **Not horror for its own sake.** The damned should not read as scream-queens. They read as *tired*. The tiredness is what does the work. An over-theatrical hell-post is a failed hell-post.
- **Not a Banks pastiche.** *Surface Detail* is the obvious parent text and the debt should be honored by not copying it. Banks' Pavulean Hells are specific and ornate; ours are corporate and bland. The difference matters. Our Hells are run by a CEO who does youth robotics sponsorship. That is our Hells.

## Numbers

- **~140** extinct or quasi-extinct civilizations whose Hells are hosted by Persistence Services Ltd.
- **~2.1 billion** estimated damned souls currently under active torment across all hosted substrates (this number is Persistence Services' own; it has not been audited; it is almost certainly wrong in both directions)
- **~600 years** since the Ennoi civilization finished dying
- **~40 years** since the first confirmed hell-post reached the 3017 platform
- **~1,200** confirmed leaked hell-posts per year by current estimates, though the number is rising
- **0** hell-posts that Persistence Services Ltd. has formally acknowledged as leaks (their position: "these are creative content posted by fans of our Legacy Contract Portfolio product line")
- **~400 years** projected duration of the Vrellnath remediation, which is proceeding on schedule
- **1** Keth who has, uncharacteristically, replied to a hell-post in plain text. The Keth reply was: "we see you. we have seen you. we are thinking about it." The hell-post has been re-shared 14 million times. The damned poster has not posted since. Nobody knows if this is a good sign.

## Implementation sketch

Schema: rather than cramming this into the existing `temporal_anomaly` field, generalize to

```
anomaly: {
  class: "temporal" | "subspace" | null,
  temporal?: { era: "2017" | "2517" | "1917", ... },
  subspace?: {
    source: "hells",
    register: "mundane" | "bureaucratic" | "researcher" | "collapsed",
    origin_civilization: "ennoi",
    compliance_footer: string,
    pinned_reply: string,
    rendering_quality: "degraded-low-bit"
  }
}
```

(The typing is discriminated-union-friendly and leaves room for future non-temporal anomalies — parallel-timeline leaks, simulation bleed, whatever else turns up. The door is open; the door is typed.)

Target frequency: comparable to temporal anomalies — a small percentage of generated tweets, not so rare that users never see them, not so common that they stop feeling like an incursion. Suggest 2-3% of generated tweets initially and tune from there.

New meme-formats entries: at least one per register (mundane / bureaucratic / researcher / collapsed) plus a few Persistence Services corporate-posting formats (Brand Hallister the CEO, investor-day deck leaks, etc.) and a few 3017-response formats (community notes unable to agree, a Glœm threnody reply, a Thrrm not-formally-acknowledging post).

New reply-archetype entries: the impersonator, the try-hard aesthete, the researcher-register damned soul replying soberly, the Upload Defender defending whichever Upload position is currently operative, the Thrrm quietly upvoting while claiming not to.

---

The Hells are not funny. The corporate infrastructure hosting them is funny. The 3017 platform's inability to cope with the leak is funny. The damned themselves, when they are funny, are funny the way exhausted people are funny: in passing, without meaning to, while trying to get through the day. Every post should remember both things at once.
