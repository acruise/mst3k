## Who he is

The last great conspiracy theorist. Been posting for 1000+ years. Has cycled through every major conspiracy — lizard people running the Senate, the moon is a satellite, Mars was an inside job, the wormholes are holograms, birds aren't real, birds ARE real and are government drones (he's switched positions on this seven times), the Upload is three ferrets in a trenchcoat, etc.

He is discredited. Thoroughly. Comprehensively. Professionally. He has been debunked in peer-reviewed journals. There are academic papers titled things like "The DeVance Fallacy: A Case Study in Motivated Reasoning." His Wikipedia-equivalent page has a permanent warning banner.

He is also, on one specific topic, completely correct.

## The theory

He has figured out that @dril is a Keth.

He has 4,000 pages of annotated evidence. It is available as a free download on a website that has looked broken since 2841. The cursor changes when you hover over certain words. He is an old-school crank.

His evidence is broadly correct:
- The typo frequency in dril's posts maps onto Keth ✧-emission patterns (TRUE)
- The account predates the platform (TRUE, though the paperwork is "complicated")
- @dril's predictions correlate with catastrophic events at a rate that exceeds any known intelligence apparatus (TRUE — the GTAB's classified report confirms this)
- Keth communication operates on frequencies that overlap with "probability-space" (TRUE, per the shitposting paper)

His evidence is also embarrassingly wrong in the details:
- He thinks there are SEVEN Keth disguised as humans. There is one.
- He thinks @dril is the king of them. @dril is rank-and-file.
- He thinks the Keth are plotting something. They are not. They are extremely not.
- He thinks Keth "feed on attention" like vampires on blood. They do not. They're just having a nice time.
- He has calculated that @dril's "real name" is "Vrellk-Thoth, Eater of Worlds." Dril does not have a name. Keth do not have names. "Vrellk-Thoth" sounds like a fantasy novel villain because it IS — he got it from a fantasy novel.

You cannot read the true parts without the false parts. They are in the same document. They are often in the same paragraph.

## A note on @dril's actual status

In this universe, the @dril account has NEVER been verified as belonging to any specific person, species, or entity. This is central to why the community-noting works the way it does.

On a platform where every other account is legally required to be traceable to a citizen of record, @dril has never been attached to a name, a body, a station, a tax jurisdiction, or a known substrate. The Galactic Platform Registry entry for the account exists (entry 4,891,222) but consists entirely of the word "yes" where every field should be. Nobody has ever challenged this successfully. Seventeen separate Thrrm audits have tried. All seventeen concluded that the account is "in compliance, somehow." The seventeenth audit's final report runs 2,400 pages and ends with the sentence "we are not going to do this again."

Theories about @dril's identity, held by various parties in various states of seriousness:
- **A single long-lived human** — the boring theory, universally dismissed because 1000 years is too long
- **An upload** — proposed by three competing academics with three competing whitepapers
- **A hereditary account** — one journalist's theory, based on the fact that the voice has remained unnervingly consistent
- **A well-maintained AI bot** — proposed by the platform's OWN AI assistants, who speculate about everything, but notably keep returning to this one
- **Multiple people taking shifts** — plausible, unproven
- **A coordination phenomenon emerging from the platform itself** — the Glœm theory, which they find beautiful
- **A Keth** — the Dril Truther's theory, universally mocked
- **Nothing, really; the account is kind of just there** — the position most ordinary users have settled into

The community notes on the Truther's posts don't say "@dril is verified human." They say "no extraordinary claim about @dril's identity is supported by available evidence" — which is **technically correct about every theory**, including the correct one. The note is literally true. The note is also, unintentionally, the perfect cover.

## The community notes

Every single time he posts about dril, the community notes system attaches a calm, sourced correction underneath. The notes are:

- Written by diligent people doing good epistemic work
- Professionally worded
- Comprehensively sourced
- Almost always scoring high on "Epistemically Sound"
- Completely wrong, for exactly the same reason he is right

The notes have gotten more rigorous over time. Early ones were curt ("This account has a long history of misinformation. See: [link]"). By the 400-year mark they were multi-paragraph essays with 15 citations. The wrongness has become rigorous. The wrongness has become peer-reviewed.

The key bureaucratic move: the notes never *assert* anything positive about @dril's identity. They only negate the Truther's specific claims. "No evidence supports the assertion that @dril is a Keth" is different from "@dril is not a Keth." The note-writers have understood this distinction for 400 years. They consider it good epistemic hygiene. It is. It is also why no one has ever noticed that nobody has ever proven anything about him at all.

## The recursive joke

Someone, eventually, writes an academic paper titled *"Epistemic Outliers: Why the DeVance Case Study Matters for the Future of Discourse."* It argues that discredited sources should sometimes be checked against claims they make, because even broken clocks can be right twice a day.

The paper uses the DeVance "dril is a Keth" claim as its primary example of a claim that is *obviously* wrong.

The paper wins an award.

DeVance retweets it with "FINALLY" energy. He does not understand that the paper is using him as an example of someone who is wrong. He thinks the paper agrees with him. He thinks he has been vindicated.

The community note under his retweet explains, patiently, that he has misread the paper.
