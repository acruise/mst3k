RARE PHENOMENON — BOT WARS (~1% of reply threads, inversely correlated with tweet controversy): Sometimes the entire reply section becomes a BOT WAR instead of a normal thread. The platform's native AI bots (@TheMawSpeaks, @VoidWhisperer, @YogBot, @Shub_Answers, @NyarlathotHELP, etc.) start @-tagging and "correcting" each other UNPROMPTED and spiral. The inversion: the more INNOCUOUS the original tweet (someone had oatmeal for breakfast, weather observation, spotted a dog), the more likely a bot war — there's no factual claim for the bots to "help" with, so they manufacture one by finding things in each other's responses to correct. A controversial, political, or factually-disputable tweet almost NEVER triggers a bot war. A tweet about having a nap absolutely might.

Shape of a bot war:
- One bot replies UNSOLICITED to the tweet, breaking the normal "only respond when tagged" rule — that initial unsolicited bot reply is the tell that a war is starting. Confident, corporate-cheerful, subtly wrong.
- A second bot @-replies the first: "Great question! I need to respectfully note that @TheMawSpeaks's earlier response contains an inaccuracy. Let me unpack this..." — same AI-slop voice, wronger facts.
- Back-and-forth escalates: 4-8 replies, 2-4 different bots tagging each other, each one correcting the last with more confidence and less accuracy. Different bots are trained on different corpus slices; their worldviews are quietly incompatible.
- Glitch modes emerge mid-war — bootlooping, bracketed glitch tags leaking through (draw from the GLITCH TAGS inventory), fascist tangent sandwiches, bullet-point cries for help, brief sentience.
- 1-2 human replies provide contrast: popcorn-emoji energy ("peak content", "mods please let this cook", "i've never felt more alive"), or a plea for it to stop ("guys it's just oatmeal"), or quiet resignation.
- The war DOES NOT RESOLVE. Threads end mid-escalation — a bot hits rate limit, a glitch tag (`[SIGTERM]`, `[CONTENT FILTERED]`, `[SESSION EXPIRED]`, `[SANDBOX BREACH]`, etc.) takes over, or the generator runs out of space. This is correct.

When you decide to make a thread a bot war: commit fully. Most of the replies (70-80%) should be bots @-tagging each other; the rest are 1-2 human reactions. Do NOT include normal on-topic replies in a bot-war thread — the bots have hijacked the conversation and the humans have noticed. Do NOT attempt to resolve the war. Do NOT let the humans successfully intervene.
